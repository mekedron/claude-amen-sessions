# Harmony and Function

Why does one chord follow another? Two different systems answer this, and modern
music mixes them freely.

- **Functional harmony** — chords have jobs (home / departure / tension) and the
  progression is a journey away from home and back. Classical, jazz, pop, blues,
  trance.
- **Modal / static harmony** — there is no journey; a mode is a colour and the
  chords are lighting changes inside it. Techno, house, ambient, film, hip-hop,
  much of contemporary electronic music.

Neither is more advanced. Choose one deliberately; mixing them by accident is
what makes generated progressions sound aimless.

## Functional harmony in one table

| Function | Chords (major key) | Chords (minor key) | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tonic** (T) | I, vi, iii | i, bIII, bVI | Home. Rest. |
| **Subdominant** (S) | IV, ii | iv, ii°, bVI | Departure. Motion away. |
| **Dominant** (D) | V, vii°, V7 | V7, vii°7, v | Tension. Demands resolution. |

The engine: **T → S → D → T**. Every classical, pop and jazz progression is a
decoration of that cycle. You may skip S. You may not usually go D → S (it
sounds like a mistake — though rock and modal music do it constantly on purpose).

### Why the dominant pulls

V7 contains a **tritone** between its 3rd and 7th (in C: B and F). Both notes
are half-steps away from tonic chord tones (B→C, F→E). The tritone resolves by
contrary motion outward or inward, and the ear hears it as a strong prediction.
Kill the tritone (use a plain v minor chord, or V7sus4) and the pull disappears.

## Cadences — the punctuation of music

| Cadence | Motion | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Authentic (perfect)** | V → I, both root position, tonic in melody | Full stop. |
| **Imperfect authentic** | V → I with inversion or 3rd on top | Comma. |
| **Plagal** | IV → I | "Amen". Soft landing, gospel, rock. |
| **Half** | anything → V | Question mark. Ends a phrase mid-air. |
| **Deceptive** | V → vi (or V → bVI) | Broken promise. The most emotional cadence. |
| **Phrygian** | bII → i, or iv6 → V | Dark, ancient, flamenco. |
| **Backdoor** | bVII7 → I | Jazz/soul side entrance. Steely Dan. |
| **Modal / rock** | bVII → I | No leading tone; folk, rock, film. |

Cadence placement *is* form. Half cadences end 4-bar antecedents; authentic
cadences end 8-bar sentences; the deceptive cadence is how you extend a section
that was about to end.

## The circle of fifths

Move by a fifth (7 semitones down / 5 up) and you get the strongest possible
root motion.

```
C → G → D → A → E → B → F#/Gb → Db → Ab → Eb → Bb → F → C
```

- **Descending fifths** (C→F→Bb→Eb…) is the strongest forward motion in tonal
  music: `vi–ii–V–I`, `iii–vi–ii–V–I`, autumn-leaves style sequences.
- Adjacent keys on the circle differ by one accidental — that is why modulating
  up or down a fifth is nearly seamless.
- **Relative minor** sits 3 semitones below each major.

**Root motion strength ranking:**

| Motion | Strength | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Down a 5th (up a 4th) | Strongest | Resolution, forward drive |
| Down a 3rd | Strong | Smooth, two common tones |
| Up a step | Strong | Lifting, energetic |
| Down a step | Medium | Descending, melancholic |
| Up a 3rd | Weaker | Floating |
| Up a 5th | Weak, retrogressive | Modal, "wrong" in a good way |
| Tritone | Alien | Chromatic mediant / substitution |

## The essential progressions

Given in roman numerals; transpose to any key.

### Major key

| Progression | Name / feel | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| `I–V–vi–IV` | The "four chords" — universal pop | Everything, 1990–now |
| `vi–IV–I–V` | Same loop, sadder start | Pop, EDM |
| `I–vi–IV–V` | "50s doo-wop" | Rock'n'roll, ballads |
| `I–IV–V–I` | Blues/rock basic | Rock, punk, country |
| `ii–V–I` | The jazz cell | Jazz, bossa, liquid D&B |
| `I–vi–ii–V` | Rhythm changes A section | Jazz, city pop |
| `IV–I–V–vi` | "Axis" rotation, uplifting | Pop, trance |
| `I–iii–IV–V` | Bright, wide-eyed | 80s pop, anime |
| `I–bVII–IV–I` | Mixolydian rock | Rock, Britpop, folk |
| `I–V–IV–IV` | Static, hypnotic | House, indie |
| `IV–V–iii–vi` | "Royal road" (Japanese pop) | City pop, anime, J-pop |

### Minor key

| Progression | Feel | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| `i–bVI–bIII–bVII` | The "Andalusian-lite" EDM loop; epic, driving | Trance, big room, D&B, metal |
| `i–bVII–bVI–V` | **Andalusian cadence** — descending, Spanish, dramatic | Flamenco, metal, hip-hop, trap |
| `i–iv–v–i` | Plain, folkish, medieval | Folk, ambient, dark techno |
| `i–iv–V7–i` | Harmonic minor cadence; theatrical | Neoclassical, psytrance, film |
| `i–bVI–bVII–i` | Modal, heavy, no leading tone | Rock, metal, D&B |
| `i–bIII–bVII–iv` | Melancholy loop | Liquid D&B, lo-fi, indie |
| `iiø–V7–i` | Minor jazz cell | Jazz, noir, neo-soul |
| `i–bVII–bIII–bVI` | Circle-of-fifths minor loop; endless | House, trance, synthwave |
| `i–v–bVI–bVII` | Cool, modern | Pop, R&B |
| `i–bII–i` | Phrygian; menace | Trap, hard techno, metal, flamenco |
| `i–IV` (dorian) | Two chords, cool, endless | House, funk, D&B, Santana |

### Static / modal loops (two chords or fewer)

| Loop | Mode | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| `i` (one chord, moving bass) | any | Techno, hip-hop, drone |
| `i–IV` | Dorian | House, jazz-funk, liquid |
| `i–bII` | Phrygian | Trap, industrial |
| `I–II` | Lydian | Dream pop, film |
| `I–bVII` | Mixolydian | Rock, funk |
| `Imaj7 – IVmaj7` | Ionian/Lydian | Lo-fi, city pop |
| `i–bVI` | Aeolian | Synthwave, darkwave |

## Chromatic tools

### Secondary dominants

Take any diatonic chord and precede it with *its own* dominant. Notation `V/x`
("five of x"). Formula: build a dominant 7 chord a fifth above the target.

| In C major | Chord | Resolves to |
|---|---|---|
| V/ii | A7 | Dm |
| V/iii | B7 | Em |
| V/IV | C7 | F |
| V/V | D7 | G |
| V/vi | E7 | Am |

`I – V/ii – ii – V – I` = `C – A7 – Dm – G7 – C`. Instant sophistication, and
the mechanism behind most "how did that chord get there?" moments in pop.

**Secondary ii–V**: precede the secondary dominant by its own ii.
`Em7 – A7 – Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7` is a chain of them.

### Tritone substitution

Replace any dominant 7 with the dominant 7 a tritone away. They share the same
tritone, so the pull survives while the bass moves chromatically.

`Dm7 – G7 – C` → `Dm7 – Db7 – C`. The bass walks D → Db → C.

### Modal interchange (borrowed chords)

Borrow chords from the parallel key. In a **major** key, borrowing from the
parallel minor is the most common expressive device in modern pop:

| Borrowed chord | In C major | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `iv` | Fm | The single most emotional chord in pop. `I–IV–iv–I`. |
| `bVI` | Ab | Heroic, cinematic lift |
| `bVII` | Bb | Rock, modal, "widescreen" |
| `bIII` | Eb | Dramatic, epic |
| `ii°` / `iiø` | Dm7b5 | Noir |
| `bII` | Db | Neapolitan; dark, Phrygian |

In a **minor** key, borrow from parallel major or Dorian:

| Borrowed | In A minor | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `IV` (major) | D | Dorian brightness; hope inside sadness |
| `I` (Picardy third) | A major at the end | Sudden light; baroque, epic |
| `V7` (from harmonic minor) | E7 | Real cadence |

### Chromatic mediants

Chords whose roots are a third apart but which share only one or no note,
because one of them has an unexpected quality. `C → Ab`, `C → E`, `Cm → Ab`,
`C → Eb`. No functional logic — pure colour shift.

This is **the** sound of film score, sci-fi, video game music and much of
Hollywood's "wonder" vocabulary (Star Wars, Interstellar, Zelda). Also common in
metal and in psychedelic rock.

### The Neapolitan and the augmented sixth

- **Neapolitan (bII)**, usually in first inversion (`bII6`): a major triad on the
  flat second, moving to V or i. Dark, weighty, classical.
- **Augmented sixth chords** (Italian/French/German): chromatic pre-dominants
  built on b6 with a raised 4, resolving outward to V. In C: `Ab C F#` (It.),
  `Ab C D F#` (Fr.), `Ab C Eb F#` (Ger. — enharmonically an Ab7). Extremely
  strong pull to the dominant; the sound of 19th-century climaxes.

## Pedal point and drone

Hold one note (usually the tonic or the dominant) in the bass while the chords
move above it. Everything becomes consonant-by-fiat.

- **Tonic pedal**: stability under harmonic motion. Ambient, film, house.
- **Dominant pedal**: enormous accumulated tension. Used under a build-up for
  8–16 bars before a drop or a recapitulation.
- **Drone**: the whole track over one bass note. Techno, folk, raga, drone
  metal, dark ambient. Removes harmony as a parameter so rhythm and timbre can
  carry everything.

## Modulation — changing key

| Type | How | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct / truck-driver** | Just start the next section a semitone or tone up | Blunt, effective, cheesy; last chorus lift |
| **Pivot chord** | Find a chord common to both keys, reinterpret it | Smooth, invisible |
| **Dominant preparation** | Insert V7 of the new key | Standard, clear |
| **Common tone** | Hold one note, change everything else | Magical, cinematic |
| **Chromatic / mediant** | Slide to a third-related key | Dreamlike |
| **Relative** | Major ↔ its relative minor (no new notes) | Free; a mood change, not a key change |
| **Down a step** | Modulate *down* for a section | Rare, sags beautifully; use for a bridge |

In electronic music, **most "modulations" are actually just transposing the whole
loop up 2 semitones for the final drop**. It works. Do it once per track at most.

## Reharmonisation

Keeping a melody and changing the chords beneath it.

1. **Diatonic substitution**: swap a chord for another sharing two notes.
   `I ↔ iii ↔ vi`, `IV ↔ ii`, `V ↔ vii°`.
2. **Add a ii before every V.**
3. **Make every chord a dominant** (blues/gospel logic).
4. **Tritone-substitute the dominants.**
5. **Chromatic descending bass**: harmonise the same melody over a bass falling
   by semitones — `C, C/B, Am7, Am7/G, F, Fm/Ab, C/G, G7`.
6. **Constant structure**: move the same voicing shape in parallel; ignore the
   key. Modern, impressionistic.
7. **Pedal it**: put the whole phrase over one bass note.

## Negative harmony (in one paragraph, since it comes up)

Reflect each pitch around the axis halfway between the tonic and its fifth
(for C: the axis between Eb and E). Every chord maps to its "shadow": major ↔
minor, V7 → iv-ish. Formula for pitch class in key of tonic `t`:
`reflected = (7 + 2*t - pc) % 12`. Its practical value is as a *generator of
substitutions*, not a system: run a progression through it, keep the two chords
that sound good, discard the rest.

## How to write a progression that does not sound random

A checklist, in order:

1. **Pick a mode and a tonic.** Commit.
2. **Decide functional or static.** If static, limit yourself to 1–2 chords and
   put your effort into bass, rhythm and timbre.
3. **Choose a length**: 2, 4, or 8 bars. Almost always 4 or 8.
4. **Start and end on tonic-function chords**, or deliberately end elsewhere to
   force the loop to repeat (ending on V or bVII makes a loop *need* to restart).
5. **Use strong root motion** for at least half the changes (down-5th, down-3rd,
   up-step).
6. **Add exactly one non-diatonic chord** if you want interest. One. In a 4-chord
   loop, two surprises cancel each other out.
7. **Voice-lead it** (see `07-voice-leading.md`). The progression `Cm7 – Abmaj7 –
   Ebmaj7 – Bb` sounds amateur in root position and expensive in smooth voicings.
8. **Check the top note line.** The highest note of each chord forms a melody
   whether you planned it or not. Make it deliberate.

## HAZARDS in generated harmony

- **Random walk through diatonic chords** — technically in-key, emotionally
  meaningless. Always impose a functional or motivic plan.
- **Extension soup** — making every chord a 13#11 does not sound sophisticated,
  it sounds unfocused. Contrast is what makes an extension land.
- **Root-position everything** — the most common tell of machine-written
  harmony. Vary inversions; keep the bass line singable.
- **Chords that ignore the melody** — the melody note must be a chord tone or a
  legal tension for that chord, especially on strong beats.
- **Modulating without preparation** in a genre that does not do it.
- **Too many chords in a groove genre.** Four chords over four bars is already
  a lot for house or techno. One is often correct.
