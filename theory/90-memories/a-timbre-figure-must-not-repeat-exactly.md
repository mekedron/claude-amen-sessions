---
name: a-timbre-figure-must-not-repeat-exactly
description: Call and response applied to a bass whose rhythm is timbre rather than notes makes it monotonous, and the measurement says the opposite
type: pitfall
date: 2026-09-01
---

Every arrangement text, this library included, says a drop is **call and
response**: two bars of a figure, two bars of its answer, the figure restated
so the listener can learn it. Applied to a neurofunk bass on `tvar` it made the
record worse, and he asked for it to be taken out - "она как-то не получилась,
короче, она только хуже стало".

The measurement had said it was working. Making both bars of a two-bar cell run
the same pair of gestures and then restating the cell - A A B A - took the
bar-to-bar correlation of the spectral centroid contour from **-0.12 to +0.86**,
which is exactly what "the ear can learn the riff" looks like as a number.

**The reason it fails is that this bass's rhythm is timbre, not pitch.** A
repeated *note* figure is a hook because each repetition confirms a prediction
about which notes come next, and the notes are the foreground. A repeated
*timbre* figure has nothing else moving underneath it: the pitch is static by
design, so an exact restatement is the same two seconds of noise again, and at
174 BPM that arrives eight times in sixteen bars.

**How to apply:** in a genre where the bass line is one held note with internal
modulation, give every two-bar cell **four different gestures**, and let what
recurs be the *vocabulary* - the same eight or ten gestures reshuffled - rather
than the sentence. Cells may share a table and a character and should not share
an order. The escalation across a drop comes from the pitch arc and from which
gestures are in play, not from restating a bar.

The general form of the error is worth keeping separately from the fix:
**a metric that captures the mechanism of a hook does not capture whether the
material can bear being hooked.** The correlation was measuring the right thing
and answering a question nobody asked.
