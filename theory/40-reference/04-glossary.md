# Glossary

## Pitch and harmony

**Accidental** — a sharp, flat or natural that alters a note outside the key.
**Altered dominant** — a dominant 7 with a raised or lowered 5th and/or 9th.
**Appoggiatura** — a non-chord tone approached by leap and resolved by step.
**Augmented** — a perfect or major interval raised by a semitone.
**Cadence** — a harmonic formula that ends a phrase.
**Chromatic** — using notes outside the current scale.
**Chromatic mediant** — a chord a third away sharing one or no note; the "film score" sound.
**Consonance** — an interval or chord that sounds at rest.
**Diatonic** — belonging to the current scale.
**Diminished** — a perfect or minor interval lowered by a semitone.
**Dissonance** — an interval or chord that creates tension.
**Dominant** — the 5th degree, or a chord built on it; the tension function.
**Enharmonic** — the same pitch spelled differently (C# = Db).
**Extension** — a chord tone above the 7th: 9, 11, 13.
**Guide tones** — the 3rd and 7th of a chord; they define its quality.
**Inversion (chord)** — a chord with something other than the root in the bass.
**Inversion (interval)** — moving the lower note up an octave; sizes sum to 12.
**Leading tone** — the 7th degree of a major scale; pulls up to the tonic.
**Mediant** — the 3rd degree.
**Modal interchange / borrowing** — using a chord from a parallel key.
**Mode** — a scale defined by which degree is the tonic.
**Modulation** — changing key.
**Neapolitan** — a major chord on the flat second degree.
**Parallel key** — same tonic, different mode (C major / C minor).
**Pedal point** — a sustained bass note under changing harmony.
**Picardy third** — ending a minor piece on a major tonic chord.
**Relative key** — same notes, different tonic (C major / A minor).
**Rootless voicing** — a chord voicing that omits the root.
**Secondary dominant** — a dominant chord of a non-tonic degree, written V/x.
**Slash chord** — a chord over a specified bass note (F/G).
**Subdominant** — the 4th degree; the departure function.
**Suspension** — a note held from a previous chord, resolving down by step.
**Tension** — a note added above a chord for colour (9, 11, 13 and alterations).
**Tonic** — the home note or chord.
**Tritone** — six semitones; the most unstable interval.
**Tritone substitution** — replacing a dominant 7 with one a tritone away.
**Voice leading** — how individual notes move from chord to chord.

## Rhythm

**Anacrusis / pickup** — notes before the first downbeat of a phrase.
**Anticipation** — arriving before the expected beat.
**Backbeat** — accents on beats 2 and 4.
**Clave** — a two-bar asymmetric timeline that governs Afro-Cuban music.
**Downbeat** — the first beat of a bar.
**Euclidean rhythm** — `k` hits distributed as evenly as possible over `n` steps.
**Ghost note** — a very quiet hit that shapes the groove without being heard as an event.
**Halftime** — the backbeat moved to beat 3 only; feels half as fast.
**Hemiola** — three units in the space of two.
**Hypermeter** — the grouping of bars into 4s and 8s.
**Meter** — how beats group into bars.
**Microtiming** — deviations of a few milliseconds from the grid.
**Polymeter** — layers with different bar lengths over a shared pulse.
**Polyrhythm** — different divisions of the same span (3 against 4).
**Quantise** — snap events to a grid.
**Shuffle** — a triplet-based swing feel.
**Swing** — delaying every second subdivision.
**Syncopation** — accenting a weak position in the metrical hierarchy.
**Tresillo** — the 3+3+2 pattern; the most widespread rhythm in the world.
**Tuplet** — dividing a span into an unusual number of equal parts.
**Upbeat / offbeat** — between the beats.

## Form

**AABA** — the 32-bar song form.
**Break** — a section with only drums, or the drum passage of a funk record.
**Breakdown** — a section with the drums removed.
**Bridge** — a contrasting section, usually once, late.
**Build-up** — the section that raises tension into a drop.
**Coda** — a concluding section.
**Drop** — the arrival of full energy after a build.
**Hook** — the most memorable fragment.
**Ostinato** — a short repeated figure.
**Period** — antecedent + consequent, question + answer.
**Post-chorus** — a hook section following the chorus.
**Pre-chorus** — a building section between verse and chorus.
**Riff** — a repeated melodic-rhythmic figure.
**Sentence** — basic idea + repetition + continuation + cadence.
**Turnaround** — the last bar(s) of a form, leading back to the start.
**Vamp** — a short repeating harmonic figure, often two chords.

## Synthesis

**Additive** — building a sound by summing sine partials.
**ADSR** — attack, decay, sustain, release.
**Aliasing** — frequencies above Nyquist folding back down as inharmonic noise.
**Band-limited** — an oscillator whose partials stop below Nyquist.
**Cutoff** — the frequency at which a filter starts acting.
**Detune** — a small pitch offset between voices, creating beating.
**DC offset** — a non-zero mean in a signal; wastes headroom and causes clicks.
**Envelope** — a control signal shaping a parameter over a note's life.
**FM** — frequency modulation at audio rate; produces sidebands.
**Formant** — a resonance peak that defines a vowel or an instrument's body.
**Granular** — synthesis from many very short grains of audio.
**LFO** — low-frequency oscillator, used for modulation.
**Nyquist** — half the sample rate; the highest representable frequency.
**Oversampling** — running a process at a higher rate to avoid aliasing.
**Partial** — one component frequency of a complex tone.
**Portamento / glide** — sliding continuously between pitches.
**PWM** — pulse width modulation; moving the duty cycle of a pulse wave.
**Resonance / Q** — emphasis at a filter's cutoff frequency.
**Subtractive** — starting with a rich waveform and filtering it down.
**Supersaw** — many detuned sawtooth oscillators in unison.
**Unison** — multiple voices playing the same note, detuned and spread.
**Wavetable** — a set of single-cycle waveforms swept by a position parameter.

## Mixing and mastering

**Attack (compressor)** — how fast gain reduction engages.
**Bus** — a channel that several tracks are routed into.
**Clipping** — cutting a waveform at a ceiling; adds harmonics.
**Comb filtering** — periodic peaks and notches from mixing a signal with a delayed copy.
**Crest factor / PLR** — peak level minus loudness; how dynamic a signal is.
**dBFS** — decibels relative to digital full scale.
**dBTP** — true peak, accounting for inter-sample peaks.
**De-esser** — a dynamic processor targeting sibilance.
**Dither** — noise added when reducing bit depth, to linearise quantisation error.
**Ducking** — reducing one signal's level when another plays.
**Dynamic EQ** — a filter that engages only above a threshold.
**Gain staging** — keeping levels sensible through a signal chain.
**Glue** — light bus compression that makes elements sound like one performance.
**Haas effect** — direction determined by the first arrival within ~35 ms.
**Headroom** — the space between the current peak and the ceiling.
**High-pass (low-cut)** — removes low frequencies.
**Inter-sample peak** — a peak occurring between samples, revealed on conversion.
**Knee** — how gradually a compressor's ratio engages.
**Limiter** — an ∞:1 compressor preventing peaks above a ceiling.
**LUFS** — loudness units relative to full scale; the perceptual loudness standard.
**LRA** — loudness range; the spread between quiet and loud passages.
**Masking** — a loud sound hiding a quieter one nearby in frequency or time.
**Mid/side** — processing the mono sum and the stereo difference separately.
**Mono compatibility** — how much survives when the mix is summed to mono.
**Multiband** — processing separate frequency bands independently.
**Parallel compression** — blending a heavily compressed copy under the dry signal.
**Pre-delay** — the gap before a reverb starts.
**Ratio** — how much a compressor reduces gain above the threshold.
**Release** — how fast gain reduction recovers.
**RT60** — the time for a reverb to decay by 60 dB.
**Sidechain** — using one signal to control a processor acting on another.
**Sibilance** — harsh "s" sounds, 5–9 kHz.
**Threshold** — the level at which a processor engages.
**Transient** — the short, loud attack at the start of a sound.
**True peak** — see dBTP.

## Production and workflow

**Acapella** — a vocal-only recording.
**Ad-lib** — an improvised vocal fragment, usually panned and delayed.
**Automation** — recorded parameter changes over time.
**Bounce / render** — writing the result to an audio file.
**Chop** — slicing audio and re-sequencing it.
**Comping** — assembling a best take from several.
**DI** — direct injection; a clean instrument signal without an amp.
**Ear candy** — small non-structural sounds that reward repeat listening.
**Impulse response (IR)** — a recording of a space or device's response, used for convolution.
**One-shot** — a single non-looping sample.
**Resampling** — rendering a sound to audio and processing it again.
**Riddim** — in Jamaican music, an instrumental used by many artists.
**Stem** — a submix of a group of tracks.
**Time-stretch** — changing length without changing pitch.
**Topline** — the vocal melody and lyric written over an instrumental.
**Vari-speed** — changing playback rate, altering pitch and length together.
