# UK Garage, 2-Step and Grime

**Identity:** swung, syncopated drums with a missing kick where you expect one,
a bouncing sub bass, and soulful vocal chops. The London answer to house.

## UK Garage / 2-step

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 130–138 (classic 132–136) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Swing | **56–62%** — the swing is not optional, it is the genre |
| Key | Minor, often Dorian; jazzy chords |
| Loudness | −9 to −7 LUFS |

### Drums — the 2-step pattern

```
kick:   x - - - | - - - - | - - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - . x | . x - x | x - . x | . x - x
shaker: . x . x | . x . x | . x . x | . x . x
rim:    - - - x | - - - - | - x - - | - - x -
```

- **The missing kick on beat 3** is the identity. The kick lands on 1 and on the
  "and" of 3.
- Hats are shuffled, syncopated, and never straight 16ths.
- Snappy, tight snares and rimshots; often a clap layered.
- Extra percussion (shakers, congas, vocal blips) fills the gaps.
- Swing applied to hats and percussion; kick and snare usually stay closer to
  the grid.

Four-to-the-floor garage (speed garage / "4x4") uses a house kick with the
garage bass and swing instead.

### Bass

- Organ bass (a filtered square/organ sound), plucky and syncopated.
- Or a deep sine sub that slides between notes.
- Or a "bassline"/speed-garage growl — distorted, wobbling, aggressive.
- Rhythm: syncopated, rarely on the downbeat, filling the 2-step gaps.

```
bass:   - - x - | - x - - | x - - x | - - x -
```

### Harmony

- **m7, m9, maj7, 7sus4** — garage inherited house's jazz chords.
- Stabs on offbeats, short, with a filter.
- 2–4 chord loops; `im7–IVm7`, `i–bVII–bVI–bVII`, `ii–V` vamps.

### Sound palette

Pitched-up R&B vocal chops, organ bass, rhodes, filtered strings, 909 hats,
vinyl, MC ad-libs, reverse cymbals, "brrr" reload sounds.

### Vocal treatment

Garage's signature: chop an R&B or soul acapella into syllables, pitch them up
2–5 semitones, re-sequence them rhythmically. Time-stretch artefacts are part of
the aesthetic.

## Grime

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 138–142 (usually 140) |
| Meter | 4/4, felt as halftime or as fast 8ths |
| Key | Minor, Phrygian; often atonal riffs |
| Era | 2002– London |

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - x | - - - - | - - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x x x x
```
Sparse, hard, often 808/909-derived. Snare and clap layered. Space for the MC.

### Bass and lead

- **Square waves and detuned saws** playing an angular, often atonal riff.
- Sub bass following the riff's root.
- The riff is the whole instrumental — 1 or 2 bars, repeated relentlessly.
- Eski/sinogrime: cold, minimal, videogame-like timbres.

### Arrangement

Built as an MC platform: 8- or 16-bar loops, minimal variation, frequent drops
to just drums so the vocal carries, "reloads" (restarting the track) as a
performance convention.

## Related styles

| Style | Tempo | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed garage** | 132–140 | 4x4 kick, huge wobbling bass, reggae influence |
| **Bassline / niche** | 134–142 | Northern English, aggressive bass, 4x4 |
| **Future garage** | 130–140 | Melancholic, atmospheric, dubstep sound design |
| **UK funky** | 128–132 | Afro/soca percussion, house tempo, tribal |
| **Garage revival / UKG house** | 130–136 | Modern, cleaner, house crossover |
| **Breakstep** | 138–142 | 2-step drums with dubstep bass |
| **Deconstructed club** | varies | Experimental, textures over grooves |

## Production notes

- **Get the swing right first.** Program the drums, then apply 56–60% swing to
  the hats and percussion only, and nudge the snare 5–10 ms late.
- The bass and the kick almost never hit together — they interlock.
- Pitched vocal chops: change formant separately from pitch to avoid chipmunking,
  or embrace it.
- Keep the low end tight and the mid-range busy; garage is a mid-range genre.
- Short reverbs and slapback delays; long reverb kills the bounce.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The pitched-up "ooh" vocal; the organ bass; the reverse cymbal; the MC saying
"reload"; the 2-step drop after a 4x4 intro; the sped-up R&B sample.

## Signature techniques

- **The missing kick on beat 3** is the genre. Kick on 1 and on the "and" of 3.
- **Swing the hats and percussion to 56–62%**, and nudge the snare 5–10 ms late,
  while the kick stays near the grid.
- **Chop vocals into syllables**, pitch them up 2–5 semitones and re-sequence
  them rhythmically; time-stretch artefacts are part of the sound.
- **The bass interlocks with the kick**, never landing on it — it fills the
  2-step gaps.
- **Organ bass**: a filtered square played as short syncopated stabs, not
  sustained.
- **Short reverbs and slapback only.** Long tails kill the bounce.
- **Grime is a platform for a voice**: 8- or 16-bar loops, minimal variation,
  frequent drops to drums alone, and the riff repeated relentlessly.
- **Reloads** — restarting a track from a phrase boundary — are a performance
  convention, not a mistake.

## Hazards

- No swing → it is just slow house.
- A kick on beat 3 → the 2-step feel evaporates.
- Too much sub — garage is bouncy, not heavy.
- Straight 16th hats.
- Chords too simple; garage inherited real harmony from house and soul.
