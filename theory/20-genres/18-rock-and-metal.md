# Rock and Metal

**Identity:** the guitar riff as the composition, played by a band with real
dynamics. The oldest continuous grammar in popular music.

## Drums

```
Rock backbeat:
kick:   x - - - | - - - - | x - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -

Punk (d-beat):
kick:   x - - x | - - x - | x - - x | - - x -
snare:  - - x - | - - - - | - - x - | - - - -
hat:    x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -

Metal (double kick):
kick:   x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
ride:   x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -

Blast beat:
kick:   x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
snare:  - x - x | - x - x | - x - x | - x - x
```

- Real dynamics: hi-hat opening and closing, ghost notes, rim clicks, dynamic
  crashes.
- Fills at the end of 4- and 8-bar phrases; tom runs, snare rolls, crash accents.
- **The crash on the downbeat of a new section** is the primary transition marker.

## Guitar

### Power chords
`[0, 7]` or `[0, 7, 12]`. No third, so they work over major and minor and survive
heavy distortion (where thirds create intermodulation mud).

### Riff construction
- Built from a scale fragment plus rhythm: pentatonic minor for rock, Aeolian or
  Phrygian for metal.
- **The riff is the song.** Verses, choruses and bridges are often different
  riffs, not different chords over the same groove.
- Palm muting creates the percussive chug; open chords create the lift.
- Rhythm is usually 8ths or 16ths with syncopated accents.
- **Chromatic and tritone motion** for metal darkness; the b2 and b5 are the
  genre's colours.

### Tuning
Standard E (E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 = MIDI 40, 45, 50, 55, 59, 64). Drop D lowers the
6th string to D2 (38) so a power chord is one finger. Metal commonly tunes down
1–4 semitones or uses 7- and 8-string guitars reaching A1 (33) or F#1 (30).

### Distortion
- More gain = more compression and sustain, less clarity.
- **Scoop the mids** for 90s metal; **boost the mids** for 70s rock and for
  cutting through a mix.
- Double-track rhythm guitars: two separate performances, panned hard left and
  right. This is the single biggest factor in a "big" guitar sound.
- High-pass at 80–100 Hz to leave room for the bass; low-pass at 8–10 kHz to
  remove fizz.

## Bass

- Follows the guitar riff, or plays roots in 8ths.
- Fingered for rock, picked for punk and metal (attack and definition).
- Distorted bass fills the gap between the guitars' 100 Hz high-pass and the
  sub.
- Layer a clean DI signal (low end) with a distorted amp signal (mids) — the
  standard modern approach.

## Harmony

- Power chords make major/minor ambiguous; the melody decides.
- Progressions: `I–bVII–IV`, `i–bVI–bVII`, `i–bVII–bVI–V`, `I–IV–V`,
  `i–iv–v`, 12-bar blues, `i–bIII–bVII–iv`.
- **Modal, not functional**: rock uses bVII instead of V for its cadence.
- Metal adds the tritone, Phrygian b2, and chromatic descending lines.
- Progressive rock/metal borrows from classical: odd meters, key changes,
  counterpoint, extended forms.

## Song form

```
Intro (riff)  – Verse – Chorus – Verse – Chorus – Bridge/Solo – Chorus – Outro
```
Metal often: `Intro – Riff A – Riff B (verse) – Riff C (chorus) – Riff A –
Breakdown – Solo – Riff C – Outro`.

**The breakdown** (metalcore/djent): a halftime, heavily syncopated, low-tuned
riff designed for physical impact. Usually the lowest, slowest, heaviest part of
the song.

## Production notes

- **Double-track everything** that should sound big: rhythm guitars, backing
  vocals, sometimes the bass.
- Drums: close mics plus room mics; the room is where the size comes from.
- Parallel compression on the drum bus.
- Guitars occupy 80 Hz–8 kHz; carve out 200–400 Hz to make room for the vocal
  and 60–100 Hz for the bass.
- Modern metal is grid-tight; classic rock breathes. Choose deliberately.
- Vocals: rock vocals sit *in* the band, not floating above it like pop.

## Hazards

- Guitars panned centre — thin and cluttered.
- Too much gain: the definition disappears and the riff turns to mush.
- All three of guitar, bass and kick fighting at 80–150 Hz.
- No dynamics: a metal track at maximum intensity throughout has no heaviness,
  because heaviness is relative.
- Programmed drums with no velocity variation or ghost notes.
- Forgetting that the riff must be memorable — technique does not substitute.
