# Funk, Soul, R&B and Neo-Soul

**Identity:** groove as the primary content, sophisticated harmony as the
seasoning, and the interlocking of parts as the compositional method.

## Numbers

| Style | Tempo | Key/harmony | Loudness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funk | 90–120 | Dorian, mixolydian, one or two chords | −10 to −8 |
| Soul (60s–70s) | 60–120 | Major/minor with gospel chords | −11 to −9 |
| Disco | 110–130 | Major, minor, lush | −10 to −8 |
| R&B (90s) | 65–100 | m7, m9, extended | −10 to −8 |
| Neo-soul | 65–95 | Extended, altered, jazz-level | −11 to −9 |
| Contemporary R&B | 60–100 | Sparse, dark, extended | −10 to −8 |
| Gospel | 60–140 | The most sophisticated harmony in popular music | −11 to −9 |

## Funk

### The one
Everything in funk is oriented toward **beat 1**. The band lands together on
"the one" and the rest of the bar is negotiation. James Brown's central insight.

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - x | - - x - | - - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - . . | - . - - | x - . -
hat:    x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x .
```
- **Ghost notes** on the snare are the difference between funk and rock.
- 16th-note hi-hats with dynamic variation and occasional opens.
- The kick is syncopated, never a simple four-on-the-floor.
- Swing 52–58%, or straight with heavy microtiming.

### Bass
- 16th-note based, syncopated, full of rests and ghost notes.
- Techniques: fingered, **slap** (thumb on the low strings) and **pop** (index
  finger pulling the high strings), muted plucks.
- Locks with the kick: the bass note either doubles a kick or fills its gaps.
- Range MIDI 28–52; funk bass uses the higher register far more than other
  genres.

```
bass:   x - . x | - x - - | x . - x | - - x -
```

### Guitar
- **The 16th-note chank**: muted, percussive 16ths on a 9th chord, played with
  a wah or a fixed filter. The guitar is a percussion instrument.
- Voicings: `9`, `13`, `m7`, `m9`, played in the MIDI 55–70 range.
- Two guitars interlocking on complementary 16ths.

### Harmony
- **One or two chords.** `E9` for a whole song is normal.
- Dorian or Mixolydian.
- Movement comes from rhythm, not from chord changes.
- When it moves: `i7–IV7` (Dorian), `I7–IV7` (blues), `ii–V` vamps.

### Horns
- Stabs on the offbeats, in unison or in tight harmony (often 3-part with the
  melody on top and a 4th/5th below).
- Short, punchy, rhythmic — a horn section is a percussion section that has
  pitches.

## Soul and R&B

### Harmony vocabulary
- `m7, m9, m11, maj7, maj9, 6/9, 13, 7sus4, dim7 passing chords`
- Gospel devices: the **backdoor cadence** (`bVII7–I`), the **2–5–1 in every
  key**, chromatic passing diminished chords, the `IV–iv–I` plagal move, and
  the "amen" `IV–I`.
- **Slash chords** to create smooth bass lines under static harmony.
- Modulations up a semitone or tone for the final chorus.

### Neo-soul specifics
- Extremely laid-back timing: the drums are behind the beat by 15–30 ms.
- Rhodes and Wurlitzer with chorus and phaser.
- Chords voiced in the MIDI 50–72 range with 9ths and 11ths in the middle.
- Bass playing melodically, often with slides and ghost notes.
- The harmony is jazz; the groove is hip-hop.

### R&B drums
```
kick:   x - - - | - - x - | - - - x | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x x . x | . x x . | x . x x | . x . x
```
Programmed, swung, with heavy ghost notes and 32nd-note hat details.

## Disco

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
```
Four-on-the-floor with the offbeat open hat — the direct ancestor of house.

### Bass
- **Octave 8ths**: root, octave, root, octave. The most recognisable disco
  gesture.
- Or a busy 16th-note line with slides.

### Strings and horns
- Rising string lines, octave unisons, glissandi.
- Horn stabs on the offbeats.
- The "string sweep" between sections.

## Production notes

- **Interlocking, not stacking.** Every part occupies a different set of 16ths.
  If two parts play the same rhythm, one of them is unnecessary.
- Compression: heavy on the drum bus, moderate everywhere else. The 70s sound
  is largely a product of tape and console compression.
- Recording live (or programming with real dynamics) matters more here than in
  any electronic genre.
- Mix warm: roll off above 14 kHz, boost 100–200 Hz gently, use tape saturation.
- Neo-soul and modern R&B mix sparse and dark: fewer elements, more space, more
  low-mid weight.

## Signature techniques

- **Parts interlock, they do not stack.** Every instrument occupies a different
  set of 16ths; if two share a rhythm, one is unnecessary.
- **Ghost notes are the groove.** 20–35% velocity hits between the main strokes
  separate funk from rock.
- **The one.** Everything orients toward beat 1; the rest of the bar negotiates.
- **The 16th-note guitar chank** is percussion with pitches — muted, short, on a
  9th chord.
- **Anticipation**: chords and bass notes arrive one 16th early.
- **Lay back or push deliberately** — neo-soul sits 15–30 ms behind the beat.
- **Vamp, do not modulate.** One or two chords for a whole section; movement
  comes from rhythm.
- **Horn stabs are a percussion section with pitch** — short, tight, offbeat.

## Hazards

- Quantising the groove to a rigid grid.
- No ghost notes — the parts become blocks instead of a groove.
- Too many chords in a funk track; the vamp is the point.
- Too few chords in a soul track; the harmony is the point.
- All parts playing on the same 16ths.
- Bass and guitar in the same register fighting for 200–500 Hz.
