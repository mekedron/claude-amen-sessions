# Trance

**Identity:** euphoria engineered. Long builds, enormous breakdowns, a supersaw
lead melody as the emotional payload, and a rolling offbeat bass that never
stops moving.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 132–142 (uplifting 138; progressive 128–134; psytrance 140–148; goa 145; hard trance 145–155) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor, almost always. A, F#, G, B minor common. Harmonic minor for the "epic" flavour |
| Swing | 50% — dead straight |
| Track length | 7–9 min (club), 3–4 min (radio) |
| Loudness | −8 to −6 LUFS |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -   (layered under the clap)
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
c-hat:  x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
ride:   x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x   (peak sections)
```

- Big, bright, layered kick with a strong click.
- Clap + snare layered and reverbed — trance snares are huge.
- Snare rolls are the primary build device: 1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32 over 4–8 bars.
- Open hat on every offbeat, always.

## Bass

The **rolling offbeat bass** is the genre's engine:

```
bass:   - x x x | - x x x | - x x x | - x x x     (three 16ths after each kick)
```

- Short notes (60–120 ms), plucky, filtered saw or square.
- Sidechained hard to the kick.
- Root note mostly, with the chord root changing every 1–2 bars.
- Add a sine sub under it for weight, or let the kick carry all the sub.
- Psytrance variant: `- - x -` per beat with a heavy triplet-ish rolling feel and
  a much longer, growling character.

## Harmony and melody

- **Simple triads and add9 chords.** Trance harmony is deliberately plain so the
  emotion comes from the melody and the arrangement.
- Progressions: `i–bVI–bIII–bVII`, `i–bVII–bVI–V`, `i–bVI–bVII–i`,
  `iv–bVI–bIII–bVII`.
- 8-bar or 16-bar chord loops, changing once per bar or every two bars.
- **The lead melody is the point.** Characteristics:
  - Supersaw, 7 detuned voices, wide, bright.
  - Sits at MIDI 72–88.
  - Long notes, singable, arch-shaped, 8 or 16 bars.
  - Often doubled an octave down or with a pluck.
  - Introduced quietly in the breakdown, then unleashed at full power.
- **Arpeggios** run underneath: 16ths, gated, filtered, often on a different
  chord voicing than the pads.

## Sound palette

Supersaws, plucks, gated pads, sweeping white noise, reverse cymbals, orchestral
hits, choir pads, female vocal (ethereal, wordless or a short lyric), 909 drums,
huge reverbs, sidechained everything.

## Arrangement (the trance template, ~9 min at 138 BPM)

```
0-31     Intro: drums + rolling bass. DJ-friendly.
32-63    Add pads, arp, atmosphere. Groove established.
64-95    First melodic hint. Energy up.
96-127   BREAKDOWN: drums out. Pads, then the lead melody introduced,
         quietly, with reverb. Vocals if present.
128-159  BUILD: snare roll accelerating, riser, filter opening, white noise
         swelling, everything crescendo. The last 2 bars: silence or a single
         held note.
160-223  THE DROP / climax: full kick + bass + lead at maximum. 64 bars.
224-255  Second breakdown or a reduced section.
256-287  Final climax, or straight to the outro.
288-319  Outro: subtract, drums last.
```

The **breakdown is longer than in any other genre** — 32 to 64 bars is normal.
Trance is the genre that most rewards patience in the build.

## Subgenres

| Subgenre | Tempo | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Uplifting / epic trance** | 138–142 | Maximum euphoria, huge melodies, harmonic minor |
| **Progressive trance** | 128–134 | Subtler, longer, groove-focused, less melodic |
| **Vocal trance** | 132–140 | Full sung topline, pop structure |
| **Psytrance** | 140–150 | Rolling triplet bass, acid lines, Phrygian/harmonic minor, psychedelic FX |
| **Full-on psy** | 142–148 | Melodic psytrance, big leads |
| **Goa trance** | 138–148 | Eastern scales, acid, 90s, layered arps |
| **Hi-tech / darkpsy** | 150–190 | Fast, dark, chaotic |
| **Tech trance** | 136–142 | Techno drums, trance energy |
| **Hard trance** | 140–155 | Distorted kick, hoovers, rave |
| **Acid trance** | 138–145 | 303 lines |
| **Dream trance** | 135–140 | Piano-led, 90s Italian |
| **Balearic / chillout trance** | 100–125 | Downtempo, sunset |

## Production notes

- **Layer the lead**: supersaw + a square/pluck an octave up + a sub sine an
  octave down, all playing the same notes, glued with the same reverb.
- **Sidechain everything** to the kick, including pads and reverb returns.
- Use **long reverbs (3–6 s)** on the lead and pads, high-passed at 300 Hz.
- The breakdown should be **dramatically wider and wetter** than the drop.
- **Roll off the supersaw below 250 Hz** or it will eat the bass.
- White noise sweeps: band-pass, sweeping up over 4–16 bars, plus a reverse
  crash at the transition.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The 32-bar breakdown; the accelerating snare roll; the "one bar of silence"
before the drop; the reverse cymbal; the female vocal about flying/dreaming/
tonight; the piano version of the lead in the breakdown; the key change for the
final climax.

## Signature techniques

- **The rolling offbeat bass is three 16ths after each kick**, short and
  plucky — a rhythmic engine, not a melodic part.
- **Stack every dimension in the build.** Snare roll accelerating 1/8 → 1/16 →
  1/32, riser, high-pass rising, reverb increasing, all at once; then a bar of
  near-silence.
- **The breakdown is longer than any other genre's** — 32 to 64 bars. Introduce
  the lead quietly there, then unleash it at the drop.
- **Layer the lead in three registers**: supersaw, a pluck or square an octave
  up, a sine an octave down, all playing identical notes and sharing one reverb.
- **Long notes, not runs.** The lead melody is singable and slow; the arpeggio
  underneath supplies the motion.
- **Sidechain everything to the kick**, including pads and reverb returns.
- **Harmony stays deliberately plain** — triads and add9s. Complexity kills
  euphoria.
- **The gate**: a sustained pad chopped by a 16th-note rhythmic gate reads as a
  rhythm part without adding a new sound.

## Hazards

- Complex harmony. Trance chords should be simple; complexity kills euphoria.
- A short breakdown — the emotional payoff needs time.
- A lead that is too busy. Long notes, not runs.
- Supersaws playing 5-note chords — mud. Three notes maximum.
- Not enough contrast between breakdown and drop.
- A drop that is only 16 bars — trance drops are 32–64.
