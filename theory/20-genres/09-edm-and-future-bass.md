# Festival EDM, Future Bass and Melodic Bass

**Identity:** the build-and-drop as the entire structure. Maximum emotional
contrast, maximum production polish, designed for a festival main stage or a
streaming playlist.

## Big room / festival house

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
```
Enormous kick, huge clap, minimal else. The drop is often just kick + clap +
a single lead sound + a sub.

### The drop
The classic big-room drop is remarkably empty: a kick, a bass, and one lead
motif — often a single note or two, rhythmically punched, with everything else
removed. Its power comes entirely from the contrast with the build.

Common drop devices: the "pitched kick" descending riff, the sirened lead, the
one-note "drop hook", the vocal chop stab.

## Future bass

### Identity
Halftime drums, huge detuned supersaw chords with heavy pitch modulation, and
vocal chops. Emotional, bright, major-key-leaning.

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | - - - - | - - - - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -
hat:    x - x x | - x - x | x - x x | - x r r
```
Trap-derived: halftime snare, fast hats with rolls, and a big open kick.

### The chords — the genre's core sound

1. Supersaw or wavetable with heavy unison detune.
2. **Pitch modulation**: an LFO or envelope bending the whole chord ±10–50 cents,
   often synced so it "wobbles" at 1/8 or 1/16.
3. Heavy sidechain from the kick, creating the "breathing" pump.
4. Voiced high (MIDI 60–84) and wide.
5. Chords are `maj9`, `m9`, `add9`, `sus2` — never bare triads.

Progressions: `IV–V–iii–vi` (the "royal road"), `vi–IV–I–V`, `I–V–vi–IV`,
`IVmaj7–V–iii7–vi`. Bright and sentimental.

### Vocal chops
Sample a vocal, slice into syllables, map across a keyboard, play the chord
progression with it. Formant-shifted, reverbed, sidechained.

## Melodic bass / colour bass

Future bass's harder sibling: the drop uses **tonal, pitched bass sounds that
play the chord progression**, rather than growls. Wide, bright, aggressive but
melodic.

## The universal EDM arrangement

```
0-15     Intro (DJ tool: drums + one element)
16-31    Verse / first melodic statement
32-47    BUILD 1: rising elements, snare roll, filter, riser
48-49    Silence / impact
48-79    DROP 1 (32 bars, with a 2-bar variation every 8)
80-95    Breakdown: strip to chords/vocal, maximum reverb
96-111   BUILD 2: bigger than build 1
112-143  DROP 2: same as drop 1 plus one new element, or a variation
144-159  Outro
```

**Total: 3:00–3:30.** The streaming era shortened everything; the intro is often
4–8 bars, and the first drop can arrive within 45 seconds.

## The build-up recipe

Stack these over 8–16 bars:

| Layer | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Snare roll | 1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32, velocity rising |
| White noise riser | Band-pass sweeping up, volume rising |
| Pitch riser | A tone or arp rising an octave or more |
| Filter | High-pass rising on the whole mix (bass disappears) |
| Reverb | Increasing wet |
| Crowd noise / atmosphere | Rising |
| Chord | Held, unresolved (often the bVI or the V) |
| **The last beat** | Everything stops. Silence. |

Then: impact + crash + sub-drop + full mix on the downbeat.

## Sound palette

Supersaws, plucks, vocal chops, orchestral risers, white noise sweeps, impacts,
sub drops, festival crowd noise, tuned percussion, pitched 808s, bit-crushed
leads, hard-synced saws.

## Production notes

- **Everything is sidechained to the kick.** This is not optional; it is the
  sound.
- Layering: the drop's lead is usually 3–6 sounds in unison, split by frequency
  (sub layer, body layer, top/air layer).
- The mix is **very bright and very loud** — top-end shelf boosts, aggressive
  limiting, and often clipping.
- Reference-match obsessively; this genre is judged on production polish.
- Stereo: the drop is wide, the build is narrowing, the impact is mono-heavy.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The one-bar silence; the "drop" that is emptier than the build; the vocal chop
hook; the supersaw pitch bend; the countdown sample; the reverse crash;
the "final chorus a semitone up".

## Signature techniques

- **The drop is often sparser than the build.** Contrast, not density, creates
  the impact.
- **Pitch-modulate the whole chord.** A tempo-synced LFO or envelope bending a
  supersaw chord ±10–50 cents is the future-bass signature.
- **Multiband upward compression** (OTT-style) at 30–60% is what makes the
  chords sound dense and "finished".
- **Vocal chops as an instrument**: map syllables across a keyboard and play the
  progression with them, formant-shifted.
- **Sidechain is the sound, not a fix.** The breathing pump is a musical
  element.
- **Build by stacking dimensions** — roll, riser, high-pass, reverb, crowd — then
  cut everything for the last beat.
- **The second drop adds one element**, or transposes up, or widens; it is never
  merely louder.
- **Layer the lead by frequency band**: sub layer, body layer, air layer, all
  playing the same notes.

## Hazards

- A build with no release, or a drop with no build.
- A drop that is louder but not *different* — contrast must be timbral, not just
  in level.
- Too many elements in the drop; big room drops are sparse.
- Supersaws playing five-note chords → mud. Three notes.
- Forgetting the low end in the build (it should be absent, then return).
- A 90-second intro in a streaming context.
