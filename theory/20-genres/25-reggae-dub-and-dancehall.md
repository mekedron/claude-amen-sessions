# Reggae, Dub and Dancehall

**Identity:** the emphasis moved off the downbeat and onto the offbeat, an
enormous bass, and — in dub — the mixing desk as an instrument.

## Reggae

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 60–90 (roots 70–80; rocksteady 80–95; ska 130–160) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor and major; often just one or two chords |
| Loudness | −11 to −9 LUFS |

### The skank

The guitar/keyboard chop on the offbeats is the genre's identity:

```
skank:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -     (on the "and" of every beat)
```
Short, muted, dry, with no sustain. Often two instruments: guitar (higher,
brighter) and organ "bubble" (a syncopated 8th/16th pattern).

### Drums — the "one drop"

```
one drop:
kick:   - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -     (rim, with the kick on 3)
hat:    x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
```
**Beat 1 is empty.** The kick and the rimshot land together on beat 3. This is
the most distinctive drum pattern in popular music.

Other reggae patterns:
```
rockers:
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -     (four-on-the-floor kick)
snare:  - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -

steppers:
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -     (militant, driving)
```

### Bass

- **The most important melodic element.** Deep, round, heavily low-passed
  (almost no content above 800 Hz).
- Plays a repeating riff, syncopated, leaving beat 1 empty or arriving late.
- Long notes with space between them.
- Range MIDI 28–45.

```
bass:   - - x - | - x - - | x - - - | - - x -
```

### Harmony
Often one or two chords for an entire song. When it moves: `i–bVII`, `I–IV`,
`i–iv`, `I–V`. The harmony is a bed, not a journey.

### Organ bubble
A syncopated pattern on a Hammond-style organ, filling the 16ths between the
skank:
```
bubble: - x x - | - x x - | - x x - | - x x -
```

## Dub

Dub is reggae **remixed live at the mixing desk**. The instrumental version of a
song, with parts dropped in and out and drowned in effects.

### Techniques

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| **Drop-out** | Mute everything but the drums and bass, then bring parts back |
| **Tape/spring delay** | Long, feedback-heavy, filtered; often pushed to self-oscillation |
| **Reverb throws** | Send a single snare hit or vocal word to a huge reverb |
| **High-pass sweeps** | Filter the whole mix upward and back |
| **Siren / sound effects** | Dub sirens, thunder, sound-system culture |
| **Fragment vocals** | A single word repeated in delay; the rest of the vocal removed |
| **Bass and drums as the constant** | Everything else is optional |
| **Live EQ manipulation** | Sweeping and killing bands as a performance |

Dub invented the modern concept of the remix, of the producer as artist, and of
effects as compositional material. Its influence runs through hip-hop, house,
techno, jungle, dubstep and ambient.

### Dub delay settings
- Time: dotted 1/8 or 1/4, or free-running and slightly off-grid.
- Feedback: 60–85%, sometimes momentarily 100%+.
- Filter: heavy low-pass in the feedback path (2–4 kHz), plus a high-pass at
  200–400 Hz. Each repeat is darker.
- Saturation in the feedback path.

## Dancehall

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 90–110 |
| Feel | Digital, sparse, riddim-based |

- **Riddim culture**: one instrumental (the "riddim") is used by many artists.
  The riddim is the composition.
- Drums: digital, sparse, syncopated; the "Sleng Teng" lineage.
- The dembow pattern (see the Latin file) originated here.
- Bass: synthesised sub, simple riff.
- Harmony: often one or two chords, or none.

```
dancehall:
kick:   x - - - | - - x - | x - - - | - - x -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - x x | - x - x | x - x x | - x - x
```

## Relatives

| Style | Tempo | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Ska** | 130–160 | Fast, horns, walking bass, upstroke guitar |
| **Rocksteady** | 80–95 | Slower ska, soul influence, bass-forward |
| **Roots reggae** | 70–85 | Spiritual, one drop, dub-influenced |
| **Lovers rock** | 70–90 | Romantic, soul harmony |
| **Digital / ragga** | 90–110 | Synth-based, 1985– |
| **Dancehall** | 90–110 | Sparse, riddim-based |
| **Reggaeton** | 88–100 | Dembow, Spanish-language |
| **Dub techno** | 120–130 | Berlin; dub chords in huge delay over techno |
| **Ragga jungle** | 160–175 | Dancehall vocals over jungle breaks |
| **Dubstep (origin)** | 138–142 | Descended from dub via UK garage |
| **Two-tone** | 140–170 | UK ska revival, punk energy |

## Production notes

- **The low end is the mix.** The bass is the loudest melodic element, often
  louder than the vocal.
- Everything else is small, dry and mid-range — the contrast makes the bass
  enormous.
- Roll the bass off hard above 800 Hz; there is no "definition" boost here.
- Drums are dry and close; only the snare gets reverb, and usually as a throw.
- The skank guitar should be almost inaudible on its own — thin, short, high-
  passed at 400 Hz — and essential in context.
- Analogue imperfection: tape saturation, spring reverb, slightly unstable
  delay times.

## Signature techniques

- **Leave beat 1 empty.** The one drop puts kick and rim together on beat 3;
  the bass arrives late and plays around the beat.
- **The skank is short and dry** — no sustain, no reverb, high-passed, almost
  inaudible soloed and essential in context.
- **The bass is the loudest melodic element** and is deliberately dark — rolled
  off above 800 Hz, no definition boost.
- **Dub arranging is performance**: mute and unmute parts live; drums and bass
  are the constant and everything else appears and vanishes.
- **Effect throws.** Send one snare hit or one word to a long echo, then close
  the send.
- **Push the delay feedback into self-oscillation**, then pull it back.
- **Sweep a high-pass across the whole mix** as a transition.
- **Riddim thinking**: the instrumental is the composition, and many vocals may
  ride it.

## Hazards

- Playing on beat 1 in a one-drop.
- A bright, defined bass — reggae bass is deliberately dark.
- Too many chords.
- Perfectly quantised skank; it should breathe.
- Modern loud mastering; this music needs dynamic range and a huge low end that
  limiting destroys.
