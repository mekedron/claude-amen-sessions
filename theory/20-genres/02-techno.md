# Techno

**Identity:** relentless, machine-driven, hypnotic. Repetition as the point,
timbre and filter movement as the arrangement. The least "musical" and most
physical of the four-to-the-floor genres.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 125–150 (Detroit/dub 125–132; peak-time 132–140; hard techno 145–160+) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Often none — a drone or a single note. When present: minor, Phrygian, or a single pitch class |
| Swing | 50% (dead straight) mostly; some Detroit techno swings ~54% |
| Track length | 6–9 min |
| Loudness | −9 to −6 LUFS |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -     (or rim, or nothing)
hat:    x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x     (straight 16ths)
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -     (optional; less than house)
perc:   - - - x | - - x - | - x - - | x - - x     (syncopated, evolving)
```

- **The kick is the track.** Punchy, distorted, tuned, with a decay that fills
  40–60% of a beat. Layered: sub sine + click + body. Often heavily saturated.
- Hats are dry, short, quantised, and often the only high-frequency content.
- Percussion is metallic, industrial, processed beyond recognition — the
  "melody" of a techno track is often a resonant filtered noise loop.
- No fills in the pop sense. Variation comes from mutes and filter automation.

## Bass

- Often just the kick's sub tail, or a single-note sub under it.
- Or a rolling 16th offbeat sub (`- x x x` per beat).
- Or a distorted mid-bass drone.
- Rarely melodic. When it is, one or two notes.

## Harmony

Frequently absent. When present:

- One chord, held, filtered, evolving over 32 bars.
- Two chords, `i–bVI` or `i–bII`, as pads far in the background.
- A stab: one m7 or dim chord, short, on an offbeat, with delay.
- Detroit techno is the exception — real chords, jazz-influenced, melodic
  (Derrick May, Carl Craig).

## Sound palette

909 and 808 drums, modular bleeps, resonant filter sweeps, industrial clangs,
field recordings, tape hiss, reverb-drenched stabs, sub drones, white noise
sweeps, distorted everything.

## Arrangement (a long-form additive structure)

```
0-31     Kick + one hat. That's it. Filter closed.
32-63    Add perc. Filter opening.
64-95    Add bass/sub. Add second perc layer.
96-127   Add the "hook" element (a stab, a sweep, a vocal fragment).
128-159  Peak: everything, filters open, most distortion.
160-191  Breakdown: kick out, pads and reverb, maybe a riser.
192-255  Return, more intense: everything plus one new element.
256-287  Outro: subtract, kick last.
```

**The unit of change is 16 or 32 bars**, not 8. Patience is the aesthetic.

## Subgenres

| Subgenre | Tempo | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Detroit techno** | 125–135 | Melodic, soulful, jazz chords, futurist |
| **Minimal techno** | 125–132 | Extreme reduction, microscopic detail |
| **Dub techno** | 120–130 | Chord stabs in huge delay/reverb, Basic Channel |
| **Peak-time / driving** | 132–140 | Loud, relentless, festival-oriented |
| **Hypnotic / loopy** | 130–138 | One loop, tiny changes, trance-inducing |
| **Industrial techno** | 130–150 | Distortion, noise, metal, EBM influence |
| **Hard techno / schranz** | 145–170 | Distorted kicks, aggressive, rave |
| **Acid techno** | 130–145 | 303 lines, squelchy, relentless |
| **Ambient techno** | 110–130 | Beats plus atmosphere, listening music |
| **Bleep techno** | 125–135 | Sub bass and simple sine bleeps, early UK |
| **Melodic techno** | 120–128 | Emotional arpeggios, big breakdowns |
| **Broken techno** | 125–140 | Breakbeat drums with techno sound design |

## Production notes

- **Distortion is a mix tool.** The kick, the bass, the percussion, and often the
  entire master go through saturation.
- **Mono-heavy.** Techno is club music; the low end and mids are largely mono.
- **Filter automation replaces arrangement.** A 32-bar low-pass sweep from 200 Hz
  to 12 kHz is a whole section.
- **Resonance as melody.** A high-resonance filter sweeping across noise or a
  loop produces pitched content — that is the hook.
- **Reverb as space, not as effect.** Big, dark, damped, and often on a send that
  is itself filtered and compressed.
- **Sidechain everything to the kick**, sometimes with a very audible pump.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The 32-bar filter opening; the reverse-cymbal into the breakdown; the single
spoken-word sample; the acid line entering at bar 96; the "no kick for 16 bars"
breakdown; the rumble kick (kick + heavily reverbed kick).

## Hazards

- Making it *interesting* — techno fails when it tries to be a song. Trust
  repetition.
- Too many elements. Four to six is a full techno track.
- Changes every 8 bars — too fast for this genre.
- A kick without enough sustain — techno kicks are longer than house kicks.
- Stereo bass — this is club music; keep it mono.
- Forgetting to make the loop *good enough to hear 200 times*.
