# Drill

**Identity:** trap's colder cousin. Sliding 808s that play a melody, sparse
menacing instrumentation, and a distinctive syncopated kick that never lands
where you expect.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 138–145 (UK/NY drill 140–144; Chicago drill 60–70 felt / 120–140 written) |
| Meter | 4/4, felt as halftime |
| Key | Minor, Phrygian, harmonic minor, Hungarian minor |
| Loudness | −9 to −7 LUFS |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | - - - x | - x - - | - - x -
snare:  - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -
hat:    x - x x | - x - x | x - x x | - x r r
rim:    - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -   (sometimes doubling the snare)
```

- **Snare/clap on beat 3** (halftime), often with a rim or a snap layered.
- The kick pattern is syncopated and irregular — this is the genre's fingerprint.
  It follows the 808, not a fixed grid.
- Hats: 16ths with rolls, triplets, and frequent pattern changes; often quieter
  and darker than in trap.
- Sparse percussion. Space is the aesthetic.

## The 808 — the melodic centre

Drill's defining feature: the 808 **slides** between notes, playing an actual
melodic line rather than punctuating.

```
808:    x~~~~~~~ | ~~~~~~x~ | x~~~~ - - | - - x~~~
```
(`~` = glide continuing)

- Glide time 40–120 ms.
- The line usually outlines the chord roots plus passing tones, moving in a
  narrow range (MIDI 24–38).
- Heavily distorted for mid-range presence.
- The 808 and the kick are locked together — the kick marks the 808's attacks.

## Harmony and melody

- Minor, dark, often just one or two chords, or none — a single-line melody
  implies the harmony.
- Instrumentation: **plucked/pizzicato strings**, dark piano, bells, flutes,
  choir pads, orchestral strings, glassy synths.
- The melody is usually **one 2-bar phrase, played sparsely**, with big reverb
  and long gaps.
- Scales with a b2 or an augmented second (Phrygian, harmonic minor, Hungarian
  minor) give the characteristic "sinister" flavour.
- Occasional dissonant intervals held deliberately.

## Sound palette

Pizzicato strings, dark piano, glassy bells, sirens, orchestral hits, vinyl
noise, reversed pads, breathy vocal textures, gunshot/reload sfx (a genre
convention with obvious content implications), sub-heavy 808s.

## Arrangement

```
0-7      Intro: the melody loop alone, filtered.
8-15     Add drums.
16-31    Verse: full beat, melody dropped back.
32-39    Hook: add layers.
40-55    Verse 2.
56-63    Hook.
64-71    Beat switch or breakdown.
72-87    Final hook.
```

Sparse and repetitive by design. The vocal carries the arrangement.

## Subgenres

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Chicago drill** | The origin, 2011–; slower, heavier, more trap-like |
| **UK drill** | 140–144, sliding 808s, dark strings, the dominant modern form |
| **Brooklyn / NY drill** | UK production style with US vocals, often samples of pop songs |
| **Jersey drill** | UK drill with Jersey club kick patterns |
| **Afro drill** | Afrobeats percussion and melody over drill drums |
| **Sad/melodic drill** | Emotional piano and vocal melodies, sung hooks |
| **Drill'n'bass** (unrelated) | 1990s IDM breakcore — a different genre entirely |

## Production notes

- **Design the 808 line first.** In drill the 808 *is* the composition; write it
  as a melody, then build the kick pattern to mark its attacks.
- Distort the 808 heavily but keep a clean sub layer underneath.
- Keep everything else quiet and sparse — drill mixes are the most vocal-forward
  in modern rap.
- Reverb: long and dark on the melodic elements, none on the drums.
- The mix should feel cold: roll off warmth at 200–400 Hz, keep the top end
  glassy rather than bright.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The pizzicato string melody; the 808 slide down at the end of every 4 bars; the
dark bell loop; the "beat switch" halfway; the sample of a well-known melody
re-harmonised in minor.

## Hazards

- An 808 that does not slide — it becomes trap.
- A regular, on-grid kick pattern — the syncopation is the genre.
- Too much melodic content; drill is defined by emptiness.
- Bright, warm mixing; drill wants cold and dark.
