# Phonk

**Identity:** Memphis rap tapes filtered through internet nostalgia. Distorted
cowbells, lo-fi vocal samples, and a Roland 808 pushed into the red.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | Classic phonk 130–145 (felt 65–75); **drift phonk 150–170**; house phonk 125–135 |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor, Phrygian; often a single-note riff |
| Loudness | −8 to −6 LUFS (distortion is the aesthetic) |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | - - x - | - - - x | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -
hat:    x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x r r r
cowbell:x - - x | - - x - | x - - x | - - x -
```

- Halftime snare on beat 3, usually a heavily distorted/clipped 808 snare or clap.
- 808 kick and hats from the classic TR-808, saturated.
- **The cowbell is the lead instrument** in drift phonk — an 808 cowbell,
  distorted, pitched to the key, playing a tresillo or a syncopated riff.
- Hi-hat rolls as in trap.

## The cowbell

The defining sound. Recipe:

1. An 808 cowbell sample (two detuned square waves through a band-pass) or a
   synthesised equivalent.
2. Pitch it to the track's key — it plays a **melody**, not just a rhythm.
3. **Heavy distortion / wavefolding** until it screams.
4. Band-pass around 600 Hz–4 kHz.
5. Slight detune or chorus for width.
6. Sidechain to the kick.

The riff is typically 1–2 bars, using the root, b3, 4, 5 and b7 of a minor scale,
in a syncopated tresillo-derived rhythm.

## Bass

- 808 sub, distorted, sliding, exactly as in trap.
- Often doubles the cowbell riff an octave or two down.
- Occasionally a detuned saw bass in the harder styles.

## Samples and texture

- Memphis rap vocal fragments (chopped, pitched down, drenched in reverb).
- **Chopped and screwed** treatment: pitch and tempo lowered ~10–25%.
- Vinyl noise, tape hiss, VHS artefacts.
- Movie/anime dialogue.
- Portuguese/Brazilian phonk uses funk carioca percussion and vocals.

## Subgenres

| Style | Tempo | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic / Memphis phonk** | 130–145 | Sampled Memphis tapes, lo-fi, slow, murky |
| **Drift phonk** | 150–170 | Distorted cowbell melody, aggressive, car culture |
| **House phonk / phonk house** | 125–135 | Four-on-the-floor, phonk timbres |
| **Brazilian phonk / funk phonk** | 130–150 | Funk carioca rhythms, chants |
| **Sexy drill / phonk drill** | 140 | Drill drums with phonk sound design |
| **Ambient / atmospheric phonk** | 120–140 | Melodic, moody, less aggressive |
| **Rage phonk** | 150–170 | Maximum distortion, hyperpop crossover |

## Arrangement

```
0-7      Intro: a filtered vocal sample, atmosphere.
8-15     Cowbell riff enters.
16-31    Full beat: drums, 808, cowbell.
32-39    Breakdown: filtered, vocal sample.
40-63    Return, heavier: more distortion, extra layers.
64-71    Outro.
```

Short tracks (1:30–2:30) are the norm — this is streaming/short-video music.

## Production notes

- **Distortion at every stage.** Soft clip the cowbell, saturate the 808, clip
  the master. The genre's aesthetic is deliberate overload.
- Lo-fi processing: bit reduction, tape emulation, high-pass everything to
  imitate a cassette, add hiss and wow/flutter.
- Keep it mono-heavy and narrow — phonk mixes are deliberately unpolished.
- The cowbell should be **loud** — as loud as the drums.
- Reverb on the vocal samples, dry on the drums.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The "chopped and screwed" pitched-down vocal; the cowbell riff that everyone
knows; the car engine sample; the anime/movie quote intro; the tape stop at the
end of a section.

## Signature techniques

- **The cowbell is a melodic instrument.** Tune it to the key and write a riff;
  it is the lead, not percussion.
- **Distortion at every stage** — the aesthetic is deliberate overload. Soft-clip
  the cowbell, saturate the 808, clip the master.
- **Chopped and screwed**: pitch and tempo lowered 10–25% on the sampled
  material.
- **Lo-fi the whole bus**: bit reduction, tape emulation, high-pass to imitate a
  cassette, hiss, wow and flutter.
- **Keep it short and narrow.** 1:30–2:30, mono-heavy, deliberately unpolished.
- **Tape stop** at the end of a section is idiomatic.

## Hazards

- A clean, well-mixed phonk track sounds wrong. Some grit is required.
- A cowbell that is not tuned to the key.
- Too much melodic content; phonk is a riff genre.
- Excessive length; keep it short.
