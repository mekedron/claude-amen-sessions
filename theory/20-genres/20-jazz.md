# Jazz

**Identity:** improvisation over a harmonic framework, with a rhythmic feel
(swing) that cannot be notated exactly. The most harmonically developed
vocabulary in Western popular music.

## Numbers

| Style | Tempo | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Ballad | 50–75 | Rubato-ish, swung |
| Medium swing | 110–160 | Swing 60–66% |
| Up-tempo / bebop | 200–320 | Swing flattening toward straight at high speeds |
| Bossa nova | 120–140 | Straight 8ths |
| Samba | 180–220 | Straight, 2/4 feel |
| Latin jazz | 180–220 | Clave-based |
| Fusion | 90–160 | Straight 16ths, funk-derived |
| Swing / big band | 120–200 | Heavy swing |

## The swing feel

- Written as straight 8ths, played as approximately a triplet: the offbeat lands
  around 60–66% through the beat.
- **The faster the tempo, the straighter the swing.** At 300 BPM it is nearly
  even.
- The ride cymbal carries it: `ding — ding-a-ding — ding-a-ding`.
- The **backbeat is in the hi-hat**, closed on 2 and 4.
- Bass walks in quarter notes, slightly ahead; the drummer sits slightly behind.
  That tension is the swing.

## Rhythm section roles

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| **Bass** | Walking quarter notes; outlines the harmony; root on the downbeat of each chord, chromatic approach into the next |
| **Drums** | Ride pattern + hi-hat on 2 and 4 + comping accents on snare and kick ("dropping bombs") |
| **Piano/guitar** | Comping: rhythmically irregular chord stabs, rootless voicings, responding to the soloist |
| **Soloist** | Melodic improvisation over the changes |

### Walking bass construction
1. Root of the chord on beat 1.
2. Beats 2 and 3: chord tones, scale tones, or approach notes.
3. Beat 4: an approach note leading chromatically or by a fifth into the next
   root.
4. Mostly stepwise; leaps for variety; range about an octave and a half.

## Harmony

### The ii–V–I
The fundamental unit. In C major: `Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`.
In C minor: `Dm7b5 – G7(b9) – Cm7`.

Everything else is elaboration:
- **Chains of ii–Vs** descending in whole steps or by fifths.
- **Tritone substitution**: `Dm7 – Db7 – Cmaj7`.
- **Secondary dominants** and their related ii chords.
- **Diminished passing chords**: `Cmaj7 – C#dim7 – Dm7`.
- **Backdoor ii–V**: `Fm7 – Bb7 – Cmaj7`.
- **Modal interchange** from parallel minor.

### Standard forms

| Form | Structure |
|---|---|
| **32-bar AABA** | 8+8+8+8; the B section ("bridge") modulates |
| **32-bar ABAC** | Common in older standards |
| **12-bar blues** | `I7 / IV7 / I7 / I7 / IV7 / IV7 / I7 / I7 / V7 / IV7 / I7 / V7` |
| **Jazz blues** | `I7 / IV7 / I7 / I7 / IV7 / #IVdim / I7 / VI7 / ii7 / V7 / I7 VI7 / ii7 V7` |
| **Rhythm changes** | AABA on the chords of "I Got Rhythm": A = `I–vi–ii–V` cycles; B = a cycle of dominants `III7–VI7–II7–V7` |
| **Modal** | One or two scales for 8–16 bars each (e.g. Dm7 for 16, Ebm7 for 8, Dm7 for 8) |

### Chord–scale relationships

| Chord | Scale to play |
|---|---|
| `maj7` | Ionian, or Lydian for the #11 colour |
| `m7` (as ii) | Dorian |
| `m7` (as iii/vi) | Aeolian or Phrygian |
| `7` (as V) | Mixolydian |
| `7` (unresolved/blues) | Mixolydian, blues scale |
| `7alt` | Altered scale (7th mode of melodic minor) |
| `7b9` | Half-whole diminished |
| `7#11` | Lydian dominant (4th mode of melodic minor) |
| `m7b5` | Locrian, or Locrian ♮2 |
| `dim7` | Whole-half diminished |
| `mMaj7` | Melodic minor |
| `sus4` | Mixolydian |

**HAZARD:** chord-scale theory tells you what will not sound wrong. It does not
produce melodies. Real jazz lines come from motifs, guide tones, enclosures and
chromatic approach — not from running scales.

## Melodic vocabulary

| Device | Description |
|---|---|
| **Guide-tone line** | Follow the 3rds and 7ths through the changes; they move by half-step |
| **Enclosure** | Approach a target note from above and below before landing on it |
| **Chromatic approach** | Land on a chord tone from a semitone away |
| **Bebop scales** | Add a chromatic passing tone so chord tones fall on downbeats (e.g. Mixolydian + major 7) |
| **Digital patterns** | 1-2-3-5, 1-3-5-7 sequences through the changes |
| **Motivic development** | State an idea, then sequence, invert, fragment it |
| **Quotation** | Insert a fragment of another tune |
| **Side-slipping** | Play a half-step away from the "correct" key for tension, then resolve |
| **Rhythmic displacement** | Repeat a phrase starting on a different beat |

## Comping

- Do not play on every beat. Space is the point.
- Anticipate: land chords on the "and" of 4 or the "and" of 2.
- **Rootless voicings** (see `../00-foundations/05-chords.md`) so the bass owns
  the root.
- Two-handed voicings for solo piano; left hand shells (root+7 or root+3) with
  right hand extensions.
- Respond to the soloist: leave space when they play, fill when they rest.

## Subgenres

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Dixieland / trad** | Collective improvisation, 1910s–20s |
| **Swing / big band** | Arranged sections, 1930s–40s |
| **Bebop** | Fast, complex, small group, 1940s |
| **Cool jazz** | Relaxed, arranged, lighter tone, 1950s |
| **Hard bop** | Blues/gospel-inflected bebop, 1950s |
| **Modal jazz** | Few chords, scale-based, 1959– |
| **Free jazz** | No fixed harmony or meter, 1960s |
| **Post-bop** | Modal + bop + freedom, 1960s |
| **Fusion** | Electric, funk/rock rhythms, 1970s |
| **Smooth jazz** | Polished, melodic, radio |
| **Acid jazz / nu jazz** | Jazz over hip-hop and house grooves |
| **Spiritual jazz** | Modal, ecstatic, extended, Coltrane lineage |
| **ECM / European** | Spacious, chamber-like, folk-influenced |
| **Contemporary / M-Base** | Odd meters, complex, modern harmony |

## Production notes

- Jazz is usually recorded live with minimal processing.
- Room sound is the reverb.
- Preserve dynamics — LRA of 10+ dB is normal and correct.
- If programming jazz: the timing and the *interaction* are what algorithms miss.
  Vary velocities enormously, place the bass slightly ahead, the drums slightly
  behind, and never repeat a comping rhythm exactly.

## Signature techniques

- **Comp irregularly.** Do not play on every beat; anticipate the "and" of 4 and
  the "and" of 2, and leave space when the soloist plays.
- **Guide tones over chord-scales.** Follow the 3rds and 7ths through the
  changes; they resolve by half-step and they make the harmony legible.
- **Enclosure**: approach a target note from above and below before landing.
- **Chromatic approach into every new root** in the walking bass, on beat 4.
- **Rootless voicings** so the bass owns the root.
- **Swing flattens as tempo rises** — near-triplet at 120, nearly even at 300.
- **Bass ahead, drums behind.** That tension is the swing.
- **Motivic development beats scale-running** — state, sequence, invert, fragment.

## Hazards

- Perfectly quantised swing — the feel comes from the deviation.
- Running scales instead of shaping lines.
- Comping on every beat.
- Voicings in root position with the bass also playing the root.
- Too many notes at slow tempos; ballads need space.
