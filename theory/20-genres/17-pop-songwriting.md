# Pop Songwriting

**Identity:** maximum memorability in minimum time. Every element serves the
vocal, and every section is optimised for the moment it arrives.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 90–130 (streaming-era sweet spot 95–115) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Whatever suits the vocalist; C, G, D, A, F major and their relative minors dominate |
| Length | 2:30–3:20 |
| Loudness | −10 to −8 LUFS |

## Structure

The modern standard:

```
Intro         0:00-0:08   4-8 bars, often the hook or a vocal fragment
Verse 1       0:08-0:35   16 bars
Pre-chorus    0:35-0:48   8 bars — builds, often a different chord centre
Chorus 1      0:48-1:10   16 bars — the point of the song
Post-chorus   1:10-1:20   8 bars — a hook without words, or a drop
Verse 2       1:20-1:40   8-16 bars (shorter than verse 1)
Pre-chorus    1:40-1:50   8 bars
Chorus 2      1:50-2:12   16 bars
Bridge        2:12-2:30   8 bars — new material, harmonic shift, or a drop-out
Chorus 3      2:30-3:00   16-24 bars, biggest
Outro         3:00-3:10   short
```

Rules the structure obeys:

1. **First chorus by 0:50.** Preferably by 0:45.
2. **The intro must contain something interesting immediately.** Streaming skip
   rates peak in the first 5 seconds.
3. **Verse 2 is shorter than verse 1.** The listener already knows the setup.
4. **The last chorus is the biggest** — added layers, a key change, an octave
   jump in the vocal, a stripped bar before it, or all four.
5. **Never repeat a section identically.** Something changes every time.

## The sections, and what each must do

| Section | Job | Melodic register | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | Establish the sound world; hook the listener | — | Low-medium |
| **Verse** | Set up the story; conversational | Low | Low |
| **Pre-chorus** | Build tension; often harmonically unstable | Rising | Medium |
| **Chorus** | Deliver the hook and the title | High | Maximum |
| **Post-chorus** | An extra, wordless hook | High | High |
| **Bridge** | Contrast; new angle; a moment of doubt | Varies | Often lowest |
| **Outro** | Exit cleanly | Falling | Decreasing |

**The pre-chorus is the most underused section.** Its job is to make the chorus
inevitable: rising melody, harmonically unresolved (often ending on V or IV),
increasing rhythmic density, and often a lift in register.

## Harmony

Pop harmony is deliberately simple and endlessly reusable.

| Progression | Name |
|---|---|
| `I–V–vi–IV` | The axis progression |
| `vi–IV–I–V` | Same, sadder entry point |
| `I–vi–IV–V` | Doo-wop / 50s |
| `IV–I–V–vi` | Uplifting rotation |
| `vi–V–IV–V` | Melancholic loop |
| `I–iii–vi–IV` | Softer, more nuanced |
| `I–V–vi–iii–IV–I–IV–V` | Pachelbel |
| `IV–V–iii–vi` | "Royal road" (J-pop, anime, city pop) |
| `i–bVI–bIII–bVII` | Minor anthem |
| `I–IV–iv–I` | The borrowed minor iv — the "emotional" move |

Modern additions:
- **The same 4 chords for the whole song**, with the sections distinguished by
  arrangement and melody only. This is now the norm.
- **Modal interchange**: one borrowed chord (usually `iv`, `bVI`, or `bVII`) in
  the pre-chorus or bridge.
- **Sus chords and add9s** everywhere, to avoid committing to major or minor.
- **Pedal points**: the bass stays on the tonic while chords move above.

## Melody

- **The chorus melody must be higher than the verse melody.** By a third to a
  fifth. This single rule creates most of pop's lift.
- Short, repeating rhythmic cells.
- The hook usually contains a **repeated note** or a **small leap that resolves
  by step**.
- Range: an octave to a tenth for the whole song.
- The title lands on the strongest beat of the strongest bar of the chorus.
- **Space:** the chorus has fewer words and longer notes than the verse.

## Lyrics

- One idea, expressed concretely.
- Verse = specifics and images; chorus = the universal statement.
- The bridge is where the perspective changes.
- Contemporary pop favours conversational, first-person, present-tense writing.
- Prosody: word stress on strong beats, always. Read the lyric aloud in rhythm.

## Production

| Element | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Vocal** | Front and centre, heavily automated, doubled in the chorus, ad-libs and stacks |
| **Drums** | Simple, big, modern; the kick and clap carry the song |
| **Bass** | Simple, supportive, often sidechained |
| **Chords** | Piano, guitar, or pads; simple voicings |
| **Hook instrument** | One distinctive timbre that appears with the chorus |
| **Post-chorus** | Often an instrumental "drop" borrowed from EDM |
| **Ear candy** | Small unique sounds every few bars — risers, reverse hits, vocal chops, texture |

**Arrangement rule:** the chorus should add at least 3 elements that the verse
does not have, and the bridge should remove at least 3.

## Subgenres and adjacent styles

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Dance-pop** | 4/4 kick, EDM-influenced production |
| **Electropop** | Synth-led, quirky, often minor |
| **Indie pop** | Guitars, less-processed vocals, softer dynamics |
| **Bedroom pop** | Lo-fi, intimate, home-produced |
| **Synth-pop** | 80s-derived, arpeggios, retro |
| **R&B-pop** | Groove-forward, sophisticated harmony, vocal runs |
| **Latin pop / reggaeton pop** | Dembow rhythm, Spanish-language |
| **K-pop** | Multi-section, genre-switching, maximalist arrangement |
| **Hyperpop** | Extreme processing, distortion, maximalism |
| **Country-pop** | Acoustic instrumentation, narrative lyrics |
| **Folk-pop** | Acoustic, communal, anthemic choruses |
| **Ballad** | Slow, piano/strings, dynamic build to a final chorus |

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The "oh-oh-oh" post-chorus; the last-chorus key change; the stripped-down
bridge; the pre-chorus that stops dead before the chorus; the four-chord loop;
the whispered ad-lib; the vocal chop drop; the "one more time" false ending.

## Signature techniques

- **Register lift into the chorus.** The chorus melody sits a third to a fifth
  above the verse; this single move creates most of pop's payoff.
- **Fewer words, longer notes in the chorus**; busier and more conversational in
  the verse.
- **The pre-chorus is harmonically unresolved** — end it on V or IV so the
  chorus is inevitable.
- **Speak the lyric in rhythm first.** Stressed syllables must land on strong
  beats.
- **Start phrases off the downbeat** with a pickup.
- **Never repeat a section identically** — add a layer, a harmony, an octave, or
  strip a bar before the last chorus.
- **The chorus adds at least three elements the verse lacks**; the bridge removes
  at least three.
- **Ear candy every few bars** — a reverse hit, a vocal chop, a riser — to reward
  repeat listening.

## Hazards

- The chorus melody sitting in the same register as the verse — no lift.
- Too long an intro.
- No dynamic contrast between sections.
- Harmony too complicated; pop rewards simplicity plus one surprise.
- Instrumentation competing with the vocal at 1–4 kHz.
- A bridge that is just another chorus.
