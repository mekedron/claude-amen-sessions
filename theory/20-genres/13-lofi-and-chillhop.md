# Lo-fi Hip-Hop and Chillhop

**Identity:** jazz harmony, dusty drums, deliberate imperfection. Music designed
to be pleasant while ignored — which makes it harder to write well than it looks.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 70–90 BPM (sweet spot 80–85) |
| Meter | 4/4; occasionally 3/4 or 6/8 |
| Swing | 55–65% |
| Key | Any; major 7th and minor 9th harmony dominates |
| Track length | 1:30–2:30 |
| Loudness | −14 to −11 LUFS (deliberately dynamic and quiet) |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | - - x - | - - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - x . | x - x . | x - x . | x - x .
```

- Soft, low-passed, no click. The kick should be felt more than heard.
- Snare: a soft rimshot, a brushed snare, or a heavily filtered sample.
- Swing 55–65%, snare pushed 10–25 ms late.
- Velocity variation is essential — nothing at full level.
- Low-pass the whole drum bus at 6–10 kHz.

## Harmony — the actual craft

This is where lo-fi lives. The harmony is jazz, played simply.

Chord vocabulary: `maj7, maj9, m7, m9, m11, 6/9, 7sus4, add9, mMaj7, 13`.
**Never bare triads.**

Progressions:

| Progression | Feel |
|---|---|
| `Imaj7 – vi7 – ii7 – V7` | Classic, warm, endless |
| `im9 – IVmaj9` (Dorian) | Cool, floating |
| `im9 – bVIImaj9 – bVImaj9 – V7` | Melancholic descent |
| `IVmaj7 – iii7 – vi7 – I` | Japanese city-pop influence |
| `im7 – iv7 – bVII7 – bIIImaj7` | Circle-of-fifths minor |
| `Imaj7 – #Idim7 – ii7 – V7` | Chromatic passing, classic jazz |
| `IVmaj7 – V7 – iii7 – vi7` | The "royal road" |
| `im9 – V7alt – im9` | Minor ii–V vamps |

Voicings: rootless, in MIDI 55–75, with the bass playing the root below.
Sevenths and ninths in the top voices.

## Instrumentation

- **Rhodes / Wurlitzer electric piano** — the genre's default.
- **Upright or soft electric bass**, playing roots and simple walking figures.
- **Acoustic/nylon guitar** with jazz voicings.
- **Muted trumpet, saxophone, flute** for melodic fragments.
- **Vibraphone, music box, celesta** for sparkle.
- **Pads**: warm, low-passed, barely present.

## The lo-fi treatment

The processing *is* the genre:

| Technique | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Vinyl crackle** | A constant low-level noise layer, −30 to −24 dB |
| **Tape wow/flutter** | Pitch modulation 0.3–1.5 Hz, depth 5–20 cents |
| **Low-pass filter** | 6–12 kHz on the master or per-element |
| **High-pass** | 60–100 Hz — lo-fi is not a bass genre |
| **Bit reduction** | 10–12 bits, subtle |
| **Sample rate reduction** | To 22 or 32 kHz for grit |
| **Saturation** | Tape or tube, everywhere |
| **Compression** | Gentle, with visible pumping on the drum bus |
| **Detuning** | ±5–15 cents on keys and pads |
| **Mono-ish** | Narrow stereo image; some tracks entirely mono |
| **Reverb** | Small room or spring, not hall |

**HAZARD:** lo-fi processing does not make bad music good. The harmony and the
groove have to work before the vinyl noise goes on.

## Arrangement

```
0-3      Intro: a spoken sample or atmosphere.
4-11     Main loop: keys + drums + bass.
12-19    Add melody instrument.
20-27    Variation: drop the drums, or change the chord voicing.
28-35    Return with an extra layer.
36-43    Outro: subtract, end on a held chord.
```

Short and loop-based, but the loop must evolve: change a chord voicing, add a
counter-melody, drop the kick for 4 bars, filter the whole thing.

## Melody

- Sparse, conversational, jazz-inflected.
- Uses chord tones plus 9ths, 6ths and chromatic approach notes.
- Leaves long gaps.
- Often just a 2-bar phrase that repeats with small variation.
- Sampled instruments played "badly" (slightly late, slightly out of tune) sound
  better here than perfect performances.

## Relatives

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Chillhop** | Slightly brighter, more produced, jazz-forward |
| **Jazzhop** | Heavier jazz sampling and live playing |
| **Study beats** | Maximally unobtrusive; near-ambient |
| **Bedroom pop** | Vocals, guitar, similar lo-fi aesthetic |
| **Trip hop** | 80–100, darker, cinematic, bigger drums |
| **Downtempo / chillout** | 90–110, electronic, Balearic |
| **Vaporwave** | Slowed 80s pop, heavy pitch/tempo manipulation, ironic |
| **Plunderphonics / sampledelia** | Extreme sample recontextualisation |
| **Chillsynth / dreamwave** | Synth-based, nostalgic |

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The anime/film dialogue sample at the intro; the vinyl crackle; the rain sound;
the "study girl" visual; the rhodes maj7 loop; the muted trumpet.

## Signature techniques

- **Swing 55–65% and push the snare 10–25 ms late.** The looseness is the point.
- **Nothing at full velocity.** The kick is felt more than heard.
- **Low-pass the entire mix** at 6–12 kHz, and high-pass at 60–100 Hz — this is
  not a bass genre.
- **Detune and drift.** ±5–15 cents on keys and pads, plus tape wow at
  0.3–1 Hz; perfect tuning breaks the aesthetic.
- **Jazz voicings played simply**: maj7, m9, 6/9, m11 — never bare triads.
- **Evolve the loop by subtraction** — drop the drums for 4 bars, change one
  chord voicing, add a counter-melody — rather than by adding layers.
- **Leave the mistakes.** Slightly late, slightly out of tune beats a perfect
  take here.
- **Master quietly** (−14 to −11 LUFS); the dynamic range is part of the genre.

## Hazards

- Harmony too simple — a triad loop will not carry the genre.
- Drums too loud or too clean.
- Too much reverb; lo-fi is intimate, not spacious.
- Perfect quantisation.
- Over-length; 2 minutes is plenty.
- Mastering it loud — the quiet dynamic range is part of the appeal.
