# Hardstyle, Hardcore and Hard Dance

**Identity:** the distorted kick as the lead instrument. The bass is not under
the kick — the bass *is* the kick's pitched tail.

## Hardstyle

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 148–155 (classic 150) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor; the kick is tuned to the root |
| Loudness | −6 to −4 LUFS (the loudest genre in music) |

### The kick

The whole craft of the genre:

1. **Attack/punch** — a short, distorted transient (0–30 ms), often a
   distorted 909 kick or a synthesised click. Sits at 100–300 Hz.
2. **Tail** — a sine or triangle at the track's root note, pitch-enveloped
   downward, distorted, occupying the rest of the beat (up to ~350 ms).
3. Both are heavily saturated, EQ'd separately, and glued.
4. The tail is **tuned to the key** — it is the bassline.

```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
```
Four to the floor, and the tail fills the space between.

### Drums beyond the kick
```
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
```
Simple. The kick does the work.

### Melody and harmony

- **Screeches** (detuned, distorted saw stabs), **reverse basses**, and big
  supersaw leads.
- Simple minor progressions: `i–bVI–bIII–bVII`, `i–bVII–bVI–V`.
- Euphoric leads in the "euphoric hardstyle" branch; dark, minimal riffs in
  "raw hardstyle".
- The **reverse bass**: a bass note that plays on the offbeats, with a reversed
  envelope, creating the "off-kick" bounce of early hardstyle.

### Arrangement
```
0-31    Intro: melodic, kickless or with a soft kick.
32-63   Build: kick in, energy rising.
64-95   DROP 1: full kick, screeches, lead.
96-127  Breakdown: no kick, big melodic/emotional section, often with vocals.
128-159 Build: snare roll, riser, silence.
160-207 DROP 2: the main event, 48 bars.
208+    Outro.
```

## The rave heritage (1990s)

Hoovers, breakbeats, piano riffs and orchestra hits are the shared ancestry of
this whole family:

- **Hoover**: a detuned saw stack with a downward pitch sweep, chorus and
  phaser. Roland Alpha Juno "What The" patch. The sound of 1992.
- **Rave piano**: a bright, hard-attack piano (Korg M1 organ/piano) playing
  major-key stabs in a minor context. Euphoric and slightly wrong, which is why
  it works.
- **Orchestra hit**: a sampled orchestral stab, pitched across a keyboard.
- **Pitched-up diva vocal**: a soul or gospel phrase sped up.
- **Amen or Think break** under a 4/4 kick.

## Production notes

- **Kick design is 80% of the work.** Build it in three layers, distort each
  separately, and tune the tail to the key.
- **Everything is sidechained to the kick**, hard — often 100% duck.
- The kick occupies 40–300 Hz almost exclusively; nothing else lives there.
- Distortion chains: soft clip → EQ → hard clip → EQ → limiter, repeated.
- The genre is mastered louder than anything else; the aesthetic *is* clipping.
- Screech design: detuned saws → heavy distortion → resonant band-pass →
  resample → re-pitch.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The emotional female-vocal breakdown before the hardest drop; the "are you
ready" MC sample; the euphoric supersaw melody over a brutal kick; the
gradually-pitching-up kick in the build; the reverse bass bounce.

## Signature techniques

- **The kick is designed, not chosen.** Three layers — a distorted transient,
  a punch band, and a pitch-swept tail tuned to the track's root — each
  distorted and EQ'd separately, then glued.
- **The tail is the bassline.** Nothing else occupies 40–300 Hz; there is no
  separate bass part under the kick.
- **Reverse bass**: an offbeat note with a reversed envelope swelling into the
  gap the kick leaves. The bounce comes from the interlock, not from a pattern.
- **Distortion chains, not single stages** — soft clip → EQ → hard clip → EQ →
  fold. Every stage after an EQ generates new harmonics; that is where the
  scream comes from.
- **Screech design by resampling**: detuned saws → distortion → resonant
  band-pass → bounce → re-pitch → repeat.
- **Emotional contrast is structural.** The melodic, often vocal breakdown is
  what makes the drop land; without it the drop is just loud.
- **Pitch the kick across a phrase** for the last bars of a build.

## Hazards

- A kick that is loud but not *tuned* — the track will feel harmonically wrong.
- Bass and kick occupying the same band without one being removed — pick one.
- No emotional contrast; the breakdown must be genuinely melodic for the drop
  to mean anything.
- Distortion without EQ between stages — the result is fizz, not power.
