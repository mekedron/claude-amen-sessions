# Synthwave, Retrowave and 80s Revival

**Identity:** the imagined 1980s. Analogue synths, gated reverb drums, neon
melancholy, and a driving pulse borrowed from Italo disco and film scores.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 80–120 (synthwave 100–118; outrun 110–120; darksynth 100–140; chillwave 90–110) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor (A, D, F, C minor); Aeolian; occasional Phrygian for darksynth |
| Loudness | −10 to −8 LUFS |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | - - - - | x - - - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
tom:    - - - - | - - - - | - - - - | - - x x     (fill)
```

- **LinnDrum / DMX / 808 / Simmons** sounds. Electronic but with real samples.
- **Gated reverb on the snare** — a big plate reverb with a hard gate at
  200–400 ms. The single most identifiable 80s sound.
- Tom fills, descending, at the end of 8-bar phrases.
- Handclaps layered with the snare.
- Simple, unswung patterns. The drive comes from the bass and arps.

## Bass

- Driving 8ths or 16ths on the root:
```
bass:   x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x     (16ths, "outrun")
bass:   x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -     (8ths)
```
- Sawtooth or square, filtered, with a short filter envelope per note.
- Octave jumps for movement (root, root, octave, root).
- Slight sidechain to the kick.
- Range MIDI 33–45.

## Harmony

- Minor, simple, 4-chord loops:
  `i–bVI–bIII–bVII`, `i–bVII–bVI–bVII`, `i–bVI–bVII–i`, `i–v–bVI–bVII`.
- Major-key variants for the brighter "dreamwave" styles.
- Chords played as **pads** (slow attack, wide, chorused) and as **arpeggios**
  (16ths, gated, filtered).
- add9 and sus2 for the "shimmer"; occasional maj7 in the softer styles.

## Melody

- Long, singable, nostalgic lines in MIDI 67–84.
- Played on a bright saw lead with portamento, chorus and delay.
- Vibrato applied late in a held note (not from the start).
- Pitch-bend into notes.
- Often doubled an octave down.

## The arp

The other defining element: a 16th-note arpeggio, usually up or up-down, on a
plucky filtered saw with a short decay, drenched in a dotted-8th delay. It runs
continuously and *is* the track's motion.

## Sound palette

Juno-106 and Jupiter-8 pads, DX7 electric piano and bells, PPG/Fairlight
choir, LinnDrum, gated snare, saw leads with chorus, FM basses, orchestra hits,
vocoder, cassette hiss, VHS artefacts, tom fills, and the "shimmer" of chorus on
literally everything.

## Arrangement

```
0-7      Intro: pad + arp, or an atmospheric sweep.
8-15     Bass and drums enter.
16-31    Verse: full groove, no lead.
32-47    Chorus: the lead melody.
48-63    Verse 2 with variation.
64-79    Breakdown: pads and arp, drums out.
80-95    Chorus / solo section.
96-111   Final chorus, layered, key change optional.
112-119  Outro: subtract, end on a held pad.
```

## Subgenres

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Synthwave / retrowave** | The default; melodic, nostalgic |
| **Outrun** | Fast, driving, driving-at-night imagery |
| **Darksynth / dark wave** | Aggressive, distorted, horror-film influenced |
| **Dreamwave / chillwave** | Slower, hazier, lo-fi, gentle |
| **Vaporwave** | Slowed and pitched 80s pop, ironic/critical |
| **Futurefunk** | Sped-up city pop and disco loops, French house influence |
| **Italo disco (the source)** | 1980s, 118–128, vocoders, arpeggios |
| **Hi-NRG (the source)** | 1980s, 125–135, driving octave bass |
| **City pop (the source)** | Japanese 1980s, jazz-funk harmony, polished |
| **New wave / synthpop (the source)** | 1979–1986, songs with real vocals |
| **Cyberpunk / industrial synth** | Darker, distorted, dystopian |
| **Horror synth** | Carpenter-style; minimal, ostinato, dread |

## Production notes

- **Chorus on everything.** It is period-correct and it is the sound.
- **Gated reverb** on the snare and often the toms.
- Analogue-style imperfection: slight pitch drift, detuned oscillators, filter
  self-oscillation, tape saturation.
- Wide stereo on pads and arps; centre the bass, kick and lead.
- Dotted-8th and 1/4 delays on the lead and arp.
- Don't over-master. The 80s did not sound loud; a little dynamic range is
  correct.
- Roll off above 15 kHz to imitate the era's converters and tape.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The gated snare; the tom fill into the chorus; the neon/car/sunset framing; the
saxophone solo; the spoken-word movie sample; the key change for the last
chorus; the arpeggio that never stops.

## Hazards

- Modern, clean production — it undercuts the aesthetic.
- Chord progressions that are too sophisticated. Simple minor loops are correct.
- Drums that are too tight and quantised without the era's sample character.
- A lead melody with too many notes; 80s leads are singable and slow.
