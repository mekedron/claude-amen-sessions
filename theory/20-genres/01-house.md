# House

**Identity:** four-on-the-floor at a walking-dance tempo, offbeat hats, a warm
looping groove built for endurance rather than impact. The root of most modern
dance music.

## Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 118–128 BPM (classic 122–126; deep house 118–124; tech house 124–128) |
| Meter | 4/4, always |
| Key | Minor is default (A, F, G, C, D minor); Dorian very common; major for disco-leaning house |
| Swing | 50–56% (subtle); shuffle house up to 62% |
| Track length | 5–7 min club edit, 3–4 min radio edit |
| Loudness | −9 to −7 LUFS |

## Drums

```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
c-hat:  x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
shaker: . x . x | . x . x | . x . x | . x . x
ride:   - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
```

- The **offbeat open hat** on the "and" of every beat is the genre's signature.
  Remove it and you have techno or disco.
- Kick: round, 50–60 Hz fundamental, 200–400 ms decay, not too clicky. It should
  feel like a heartbeat, not a punch.
- Clap over snare, or clap+snare layered. Wide, with a short room reverb.
- Percussion (congas, shakers, rims, tambourine) is what makes house feel alive.
  Add one layer every 16 bars.
- Slight swing (52–56%) on the hats only.

## Bass

- Rolling 16ths, offbeat 8ths, or syncopated funk lines.
- Sidechained to the kick — 5–10 dB, release ~1/16.
- Range MIDI 33–45. Round, filtered saw or a sine with a little saturation.
- Deep house: sparse, dubby, long notes with space.
- Tech house: short, plucky, rhythmic; the bass *is* the groove.
- Classic pattern:
```
bass:   - - x x | - - x x | - - x x | - - x x     (offbeat 8ths)
bass:   x - x x | - x - x | x - x x | - x - -     (funk-derived)
```

## Harmony

- **m7, m9, maj7, 7sus4** — never bare triads.
- 2–4 chords, looping. Classic loops: `i–iv`, `im7–IVmaj7` (Dorian),
  `i–bVII–bVI–bVII`, `ii–V` vamps, `Im7–IVm7`.
- Rootless voicings in the MIDI 55–75 range, played as stabs or held pads.
- The **organ/piano stab** on the offbeat is a defining sound: a chord played on
  the "and" of a beat, short, with the reverb tail cut.

## Sound palette

Rhodes and electric piano, filtered disco/soul samples, Juno-style pads, organ
stabs, vocal one-shots ("yeah", "come on", diva phrases), 909 drums, congas,
vinyl noise, filtered strings, saxophone (unironically, in some corners).

## Arrangement (128 bars ≈ 6 min at 124 BPM)

```
0-15    Drums only (DJ intro). Kick, hats, one perc.
16-31   Add bass. Filter opening slowly.
32-47   Add chords. First groove statement.
48-63   Add vocal / lead. Full groove.
64-79   Breakdown: drums out or reduced, chords and vocal exposed, reverb up.
80-95   Build: filter opens, perc returns, riser.
96-127  Main section: everything. Small variations every 8 bars.
128-159 Outro: subtract layers, drums last.
```

The house arrangement is **additive then subtractive**, not build–drop. There is
no "drop" in classic house; there is a long, patient accumulation.

## Subgenres

| Subgenre | Tempo | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Chicago / classic** | 118–125 | 707/909 drums, raw, simple, acid influence |
| **Deep house** | 118–124 | Lush m9 chords, sparse, soulful, dubby |
| **Tech house** | 124–128 | Minimal, percussive, techno drums with house groove |
| **Progressive house** | 124–128 | Long builds, big breakdowns, melodic |
| **Future house** | 124–128 | Metallic, pitched-bass drops, EDM structure |
| **French house / filter house** | 120–128 | Filtered disco loops, phaser, heavy compression |
| **Acid house** | 120–130 | 303 bassline is the lead |
| **Garage house / soulful** | 120–126 | Real vocals, gospel chords, live feel |
| **Afro house** | 118–125 | Polyrhythmic percussion, tribal, long |
| **Amapiano** | 110–118 | Log drum, shakers, jazzy chords (see its own file) |
| **Microhouse / minimal** | 120–128 | Clicks, tiny sounds, extreme subtraction |
| **Disco house / nu-disco** | 115–125 | Live bass, strings, real disco DNA |
| **Melodic house & techno** | 120–126 | Emotional pads, long-form, arpeggios |
| **Jackin' house** | 122–128 | Bouncy, filtered vocal chops, groove-forward |
| **Bass house** | 126–130 | Aggressive mid-bass, dubstep influence |

## Production notes

- **Groove over impact.** Nothing should shock; everything should loop forever.
- The kick and bass relationship *is* the mix. Get it right and the rest is
  decoration.
- Sample sources: 70s–80s disco, soul, funk, jazz. Filter them heavily, pitch
  them, loop 1–2 bars.
- Sidechain the pads too, not just the bass — the whole track breathes.
- Keep the mix warm: saturation, tape emulation, gentle high-shelf rolloff above
  14 kHz.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The filtered disco loop; the "yeah" vocal sample; the offbeat organ stab; the
16-bar filter sweep; the piano riff in the breakdown; the "Can you feel it"
spoken word; the diva vocal about house music itself.

## Hazards

- Too much going on. House is a subtraction genre.
- A kick that is too clicky or too short — it becomes techno.
- A build-and-drop structure — that is EDM, not house.
- Bare triads instead of 7ths and 9ths.
- No percussion layer, so the loop feels rigid.
- Forgetting the DJ intro/outro.
