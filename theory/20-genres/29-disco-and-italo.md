# Disco, Italo, Hi-NRG and Nu-Disco

**Identity:** the four-on-the-floor kick with an orchestra on top. The direct
ancestor of house, techno and most modern dance music.

## Disco (1974–1980)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 110–130 (sweet spot 118–124) |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Major and minor; lush, jazz-influenced harmony |
| Loudness | −11 to −9 LUFS |

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
o-hat:  - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
c-hat:  x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -
```
Played by a human drummer, with real dynamics, fills, and a slight push. The
open hat on the offbeat is the element that house inherited.

### Bass
- **Octave 8ths** — the definitive disco bassline: root, octave, root, octave.
```
bass:   x - x - | x - x - | x - x - | x - x -     (alternating octaves)
```
- Or busy 16th-note lines with slides, ghost notes and chromatic passing tones.
- Electric bass, fingered, bright, compressed.

### Guitar
- 16th-note muted "chank" on 9th and m7 chords, exactly as in funk.
- Wah-wah, phaser, or a fixed filter.
- Often two guitars interlocking.

### Strings and horns
- **Rising string lines** and octave unisons.
- Glissandi and "sweeps" at section changes.
- Horn stabs on offbeats and at phrase ends.
- The string arrangement is what separates disco from funk.

### Harmony
- More sophisticated than most dance music: `maj7, m7, m9, 13, sus4`, ii–V
  motion, modulations.
- Progressions: `im7–IVm7` (Dorian vamp), `ii–V–I`, `I–vi–ii–V`,
  `im7–bVII–bVI–V`.
- Frequent key changes for the final section.

### Arrangement
```
0-15    Intro: drums, bass, guitar groove.
16-31   Verse: vocals enter.
32-47   Chorus.
48-63   Instrumental break: strings, horns, percussion feature.
64-79   Breakdown: bass and drums only ("the break") — the part DJs looped.
80-95   Build back with strings.
96-127  Final chorus and vamp, extended, with ad-libs.
```
Long 12" mixes extended the instrumental break — which is literally how hip-hop
and house began.

## Italo disco (1980–1988)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 118–128 |
| Character | Synthetic, melodic, naive, melancholic |

- Drum machines (Linn, 808, 909) replace the live band.
- **Arpeggiated synth bass** in 16ths, often with a filter envelope per note.
- Big, simple, memorable synth melodies — usually minor and slightly sad.
- Vocoders and heavily accented English vocals.
- Progressions: `i–bVI–bIII–bVII`, `i–bVII–bVI–bVII`.
- Handclaps, orchestra hits, and a lot of chorus on everything.

## Hi-NRG (1982–1990)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 125–140 |
| Character | Fast, driving, camp, euphoric |

- Relentless octave 8th or 16th synth bass.
- Four-on-the-floor with a very prominent offbeat open hat.
- Big diva vocals.
- Direct ancestor of trance's rolling bass and of much European dance music.

## Nu-disco / disco house (2000s–)

- Disco's harmony and instrumentation with modern production.
- Sidechain compression borrowed from house.
- Filtered loops from actual disco records (French house).
- Live bass and guitar over programmed drums.
- Tempo 110–125.

## French house / filter house

- Loop a 1–2 bar section of a disco record.
- Apply a **slowly moving low-pass or band-pass filter** over 8–32 bars — that
  automation *is* the arrangement.
- Heavy compression, often with audible pumping.
- Phaser on the loop.
- Add a 909 kick and hats over the top.
- Vocal chops or a pitched-up vocal hook.

## Production notes

- **The groove is played, not programmed.** Even in Italo, the parts breathe.
- Disco mixes are wide and bright with real dynamic range — do not master this
  material to modern loudness.
- The bass and kick are the foundation, but the *strings and guitars* are what
  make it disco.
- Percussion (congas, tambourine, cowbell, shaker) fills the 16ths.
- Reverb: plate on vocals and snare, room on drums, generous on strings.
- Tape saturation and console warmth are the era's sound.

## Clichés (use knowingly)

The string glissando into the chorus; the octave bassline; the handclaps on 2
and 4; the "get up" vocal; the filter sweep; the breakdown to bass and drums;
the key change for the final chorus; the cowbell.

## Hazards

- Programming the bassline with no dynamics or slides.
- Forgetting the string/horn arrangement — without it this is just house.
- Modern loudness mastering.
- Simplistic harmony; disco used real chord vocabulary.
- Missing the offbeat open hat.
