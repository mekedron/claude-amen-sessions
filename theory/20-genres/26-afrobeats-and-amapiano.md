# Afrobeats, Amapiano and African Popular Music

**Identity:** polyrhythmic percussion, relaxed tempos, and a groove built from
many light interlocking parts rather than a few heavy ones.

## Afrobeats (contemporary West African pop)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 100–115 |
| Meter | 4/4 with strong triplet/swing inflection |
| Key | Major and minor; simple 4-chord loops |
| Loudness | −9 to −8 LUFS |

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | - - x - | - - x - | - - - -
snare:  - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -   (often a rim or clap)
shaker: x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x .
conga:  - - x - | x - - x | - - x - | x - - -
perc:   - x - - | - - x - | - x - - | - - x -
```

- The groove comes from **layered percussion**, not from a big kick and snare.
- Shakers, congas, woodblocks, log drums, talking drum, clave-like patterns.
- **Swing/triplet inflection**: many afrobeats patterns sit between straight and
  triplet — around 56–60% swing, applied unevenly.
- The kick is soft and round, not a club kick.

### Bass
- Round, melodic, syncopated. Often plays a repeating riff with the guitar.
- Sub-heavy but not overwhelming.
- Frequently anticipates the downbeat.

### Harmony
- Simple 4-chord loops: `I–V–vi–IV`, `vi–IV–I–V`, `i–bVII–bVI–bVII`,
  `I–IV–I–V`.
- Guitar plays a **highlife-derived arpeggiated figure** — clean, bright,
  chorused, in 8ths or 16ths.
- Keys: simple pads, marimba, plucked synths.

### Vocals
- Melodic, conversational, with pidgin/local-language phrasing.
- Heavy use of call-and-response and group vocal responses.
- Ad-libs constantly.

## Amapiano (South African)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 108–118 |
| Meter | 4/4 |
| Key | Minor, jazzy 7th and 9th chords |
| Length | 5–8 minutes |
| Loudness | −10 to −8 LUFS |

### Drums
```
kick:   x - - - | x - - - | x - - - | x - - -
clap:   - - - - | x - - - | - - - - | x - - -
hat:    - - x - | - - x - | - - x - | - - x -
shaker: x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x
```
Four-on-the-floor but soft; the percussion carries everything.

### The log drum
The genre's signature: a deep, pitched, percussive bass sound with a fast
pitch-envelope drop, playing a syncopated melodic riff.

```
log:    - - x - | x - - x | - - x - | x - - -
```
- Design: a sine or triangle with a rapid downward pitch envelope (like a short
  808), tuned to the key, slightly distorted, with a click transient.
- It functions simultaneously as bass, percussion and hook.

### Harmony
- **Jazzy**: m7, m9, maj7, 11ths. Piano and Rhodes-led.
- Long, evolving chord progressions — often 8 or 16 bars.
- Deep house heritage is audible.

### Arrangement
- Long tracks with slow development, closer to house than to pop.
- Percussion builds, vocal fragments, log drum drops in and out.
- Space and patience.

## Polyrhythm — the underlying principle

Much West and Central African music is organised around a **bell pattern** (a
timeline) with parts in different metric relationships to it.

Standard 12/8 bell pattern (the "short bell" / Ewe):
```
12 pulses: x - x - x x - x - x - x
```
Over this, drums play patterns in 3, 4 and 6 groupings simultaneously. The
listener can hear the same music "in 4" or "in 3" depending on where they place
the downbeat — this ambiguity is the point, not a problem to be resolved.

**For programming:** put one layer on a 3-step cycle, one on 4, one on 6, over a
12-step bar. The result is a groove that never quite repeats within a bar.

## Production notes

- **Many quiet layers beat few loud ones.** Afrobeats and amapiano mixes have
  10–20 percussion elements, each at −18 to −25 dB.
- Pan the percussion widely and keep the kick, bass and vocal centred.
- Percussion needs real dynamic variation and slight timing looseness.
- The low end is present but not dominant; these are groove genres, not
  weight genres — except for the amapiano log drum, which is the exception.
- Keep the mix warm and slightly soft; harshness at 3–5 kHz is out of place.

## Signature techniques

- **Many quiet layers, not few loud ones.** Ten to twenty percussion elements at
  −18 to −25 dB, each on a different subdivision.
- **Swing unevenly.** These grooves sit between straight and triplet, around
  56–60%, and not applied uniformly to every part.
- **The kick is soft.** It anchors without dominating; the percussion carries the
  energy.
- **Polyrhythm as structure**: layers on 3-, 4- and 6-step cycles over a 12-step
  bar, so the same music can be heard "in 3" or "in 4".
- **The log drum is bass, percussion and hook at once** — a pitched sine with a
  fast downward pitch envelope, playing a syncopated melodic riff.
- **Amapiano is patient.** 5–8 minutes, slow accumulation, closer to deep house
  than to pop.
- **Guitar plays a highlife arpeggio figure**, clean and bright, rather than
  chords.
- **Group vocal responses** answer every lead phrase.

## Hazards

- Treating polyrhythm as decoration rather than structure.
- A four-on-the-floor kick that is too hard — these kicks are soft.
- Too few percussion layers.
- Quantising everything to a rigid grid.
- Using "African" as a single aesthetic; these are dozens of distinct traditions
  with their own rules, instruments and histories.
